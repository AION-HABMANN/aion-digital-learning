# DL Standard: how a Digital Learning day is built

**This is the living rulebook for the DL course.** It holds the current standard, the history of every change, and which day already follows which version. When a day is adjusted and the change should apply elsewhere, it is changed **here first**, a line is added to the changelog, and the status matrix shows which days still need it.

- **Current version: v1.5** (2026-10-18), first written from the rebuild of Day 2; v1.2 added the pixel story comics (section 13), v1.3 the gap audit of the case (section 14), v1.4 the guided table (section 15), v1.5 the Route 2 windows that look like the real tools (section 4).
- **Reference implementation: Day 2** (`playground-dl/`, commits `dafc57f`, `f008ac4`, `3555e6b`, `ff322a3`).
- **Read this file first for any DL task.** `CLAUDE.md` (#1 to #56) still holds the older rules; where they differ for a task, this file wins (section 10).
- **Scope: the website only.** The Word documents follow their own rule (CLAUDE.md #53) and are touched only on the user's signal.

## How this file is kept up to date

1. A change is decided in a session (the user adjusts something on a day, or asks for a rule).
2. The rule is edited **in section 1 to 9 or 13 to 15 of this file**, never only in a code comment or in chat.
3. A line goes into the **changelog (section 11)**: date, what changed, why, and which days it applies to (`all days`, `from Day N`, `Day N only`).
4. The **status matrix (section 12)** is updated: the day that got the change is marked, the others stay open.
5. The version number goes up (v1.1, v1.2, …) when a rule changes meaning; a wording fix does not bump it.
6. The assistant offers to apply the change to the days that are still open, one day at a time; nothing is applied to other days without the user's yes.

## 1. Principles

- **The curriculum's own questions are the tasks.** Each numbered item of the plan column (Strukturplan) becomes a question, in the plan's order and close to its words. Nothing is put in front of them and nothing replaces them. Level 1 Task 1 is Block 1.1.
- **Free answers.** Open questions of the plan are text, never a pick from a list, a sort board, a rating grid or a live panel. The only fixed choices are the ones the plan itself offers (platform A or B; yes, no or partly; option A, B or C).
- **Every block is Core.** There are no Optional blocks. A block either is a plan item or it does not exist. An old instrument that matches no plan item is removed.
- **No figure the plan does not print.** No cost, weeks, budget, score or threshold is invented. What the case needs beyond the plan stays minimal and is labelled "Case assumption".
- **Case detail closes gaps, never answers.** Where the plan says only "limited" or "unclear", the case brief adds qualitative Case assumptions and a "known and open" box, never a figure; the audit and its checks are section 14.
- **Checks are completeness only**: an empty field, a list with too few items, a text that is too short, each as a named item. A choice that differs from the model is never missing. Nothing is marked right or wrong.

## 2. Fields: form by default, table as a quiet extra, fields that grow

- A list of N items is **one text box, one item per line** by default. Where the plan asks for several things per item (risk, benefit, effort; what, how, measure), the default is **one box per item**.
- A **table** (rows by columns) is offered through **one quiet text link under the field**, never a prominent button; where the field has a guided table (section 15) the link reads "Not sure how to fill this in? Use the guided table." Both layouts are kept, only the chosen one counts, and a switch never loses the other. Shared component: `components/ui/FormOrTable.tsx`; the five-row case of Block 1.1 is built in `components/day2/Task1.tsx`.
- **Every free-text field and every table cell is `components/ui/AutoTextarea.tsx`**: it starts small and grows with what is typed (like a Google Docs cell); no clipped answer, no inner scrollbar, no single-line `<input>` for an answer. `TextBox` (`components/ui/Inputs.tsx`) already uses it. A truly short value (the participant's name, the mentor passcode) stays an `<input>`.
- **A rating scale is a recommendation, never a requirement.** Where the plan says "assess" and gives no scale, the help may say that teams often rate Low, Medium or High with a reason, and the model answer may use it; a learner who writes plain sentences is just as complete, and nothing checks for the words. The Materi says what practice looks like (a coarse scale or a value-effort two-by-two without data, a scoring formula with data) and that the reason carries the decision.
- Every free-text field keeps: a collapsed "How to build the answer" (`WritingHelp`, CLAUDE.md #55 section 1), an example answer on a different company (`ExampleAnswer`, #23), a mentor model answer in EN and DE, and an entry in the missing list (#1, #34).

## 3. Plan rows with no written deliverable: a hidden facilitator card

The Coaching row (UE4) and the Feedbackrunde (UE7) have questions to discuss and nothing to write. They are **not tasks**. On the page, in the place of the row, there is a card that stays hidden until the mentor passcode is entered; then one quiet button opens it. It holds the plan's coaching points and questions, has no fields, and is not in the missing list, the ring, the page map or the export. Reference: `CoachingCard` in `components/day2/Task1.tsx`.

## 4. Working simulations (CLAUDE.md #60 is the stub of this section)

Every platform, option and case artefact a task asks the learner to judge is **also** something the learner can open and use, so that a senior learner judges from experience, not from a description. Authentic tasks and contexts (Herrington, Reeves and Oliver, *A Guide to Authentic e-Learning*, 2010) and simulation research (what transfers is mostly task and functional realism, more than a photo-real look) are the basis: **it works like the real thing; it looks plausible.**

- **Two layers:** the overview card or schematic in the block, and an **"Open … and try it"** button that opens a simulation in a dialog with a browser frame (`components/sim/SimFrame.tsx`, `SimButton`: address bar, Reset, Close, Escape, focus kept inside, a footer that says it is a simulation).
- **One to one from the plan's clues.** Clear path, progress indicator, short units, long content, no navigation, no feedback: each becomes something the learner experiences. Every numbered fact the task prints can be found in it.
- **Nothing is added that changes the answer**: no extra problem, no extra virtue, no hint, no "bad" styling, no marking. A plain, neutral screen.
- **Options are experiences too** (Day 2 Block 1.2: a high-fidelity prototype in which every drawn screen is linked, with nothing real behind it (the quiz result is fixed, nothing is saved); a paper prototype paged by a person who plays the computer; the live product).
- **The case company is a place to visit** (LearnPro today is the same simulation as Platform B; Route 2 has the manager's desk with the note, the competitor news and the table of what is recorded). A figure appears only where the plan prints one.
- Own colours, never the site's blue, teal and rust; state is session-only, never counted, never exported; built inline, no external site; EN and DE by hand; 390 px without horizontal scroll. Day 2's simulations: `components/day2/sims.tsx`.
- **Route 2 windows look like the real tools (user decision 2026-10-18, standard for every DL day from Day 2; Day 1's own Route 2 form excepted).** Every button of Route 2 that opens a simulation opens something that looks and behaves like the tool a person in that role would really use, not a list of sentences. **The desk is a mail client** (`components/sim/DeskSim.tsx`): sender avatars and addresses, times, unread marks, and attachments that open in place (the table of what the platform records as a spreadsheet with column letters, a news article as a page, the product as a link to the live course). **A second, optional button, "Observe the platform for real"**, opens the case platform as it is today, in tabs (`components/sim/ObserveKit.tsx`, per day `components/dayN/observe.tsx`, slot `observe` in the Route 2 block; Day 2 and Day 3 in their own Task2): the product itself (the Route 1 simulation; Day 2 as two different learners, Day 10 on a phone and a desktop); **a support ticket tool** with the users' own words, the time, the account and what the tool captured about each ticket (channel, device, page), where what was not captured says "not captured"; **an analytics console** whose reports show figures as bars with the number at the end and, where it is a log, its rows, while a report the platform does not have is greyed with "no data" and says so; and, where the brief has competitors, **a public competitor page** with its claims, a customer quote and a demo (Days 2 and 4). A learner who wants to analyse something real opens it; one who is content with the text just writes. **Rules.** Everything is a Case assumption made up from the brief's facts and the desk's data table, and every window says so. **Figures may appear in these windows only**, never in a task, a check, a model answer or an export, and only for what the brief says is recorded; what the brief says is missing stays missing (no report, "not captured"). No verdict, no ranking, no total added up for the reader, no highlighted "finding", and nothing that reveals an answer; the voices differ and do not agree. The text of the task stays sufficient, so nothing here is required. The earlier rule that nothing may be added to a case is relaxed by the user for these windows: observations may be richer, but they stay neutral and within the direction of the plan.
- **Every button in a simulation shows a real view (user rule 2026-10-18).** A control that looks like a button or a list entry must lead somewhere a real product would: a course in a list opens that course, a menu tile opens its page with real content, "Next" opens the next lesson with its own text, "Save" answers. No dead taps, no "not built yet", no "the page opens here"; a thin or placeholder view counts as a defect, because a learner reads it as a hint that the option is poor. The one exception is a control whose silence is the plan's own clue (Day 10: a completed task gets no response); it is kept silent on purpose, in the platform as the plan describes it and in an option that does not change it. Check it by scanning `components/day*/sims.tsx` and `observe.tsx` for a `<button` without `onClick`, and by clicking every control of every window once. Shared lesson texts for paging: `components/sim/lessonText.ts`.
- A day's simulations are built directly from the plan's clues (v1.1: no demo-and-wait step on a full upgrade).

## 5. Real pictures (CLAUDE.md #52)

What has a form is shown in a real picture, the same example once per variant, interactive where a change is the lesson; the words beside it only say what to look at. A task option that names a visual kind carries a thumbnail. A new picture is built directly from the plan's clues (v1.1); a chat mockup only if the user asks for one.

## 6. The Materi follows the tasks (done when the user asks)

- Every "why" line and rule names the blocks that really exist.
- No card teaches a rule or a figure the tasks no longer use (an effort rule by cost, a budget).
- A worked example uses its own company **and its own options**, so the task's answer is never shown.
- Read the cards against the plan's *Wissen* bullets and the Level 3 input lines one by one; close each gap with one sentence, and say when a statement is the plan's view and not a separate finding.
- A card that a block draws on is Core. The task chips (`MaterialRefs`) list every card a block needs.
- The Materi is **not touched** while tasks are being rebuilt unless the user asks.

## 7. State and checks

- One slice per route; both layouts of a list kept, only the chosen one counted; the persist version bumped; the deep `mergeDefaults` fills new fields; an old blob is tested (CLAUDE.md #9).
- `npx tsc --noEmit` and `npm run verify:dayN` (the model fill leaves nothing missing, each layout counts alone, a different decision exports, no Optional block, an old blob loads).
- The browser from a clean `localStorage` in EN and DE at 1280 px and 390 px, with a very long answer in every box and cell, the mentor fill and the export. Never run `next build` while the dev server runs (CLAUDE.md #10).

## 8. Word documents

Only on the user's signal (CLAUDE.md #53, #31). Order: the `_source` Markdown (Materi, Task, Trainer), then the build tools in `materi-task-docx/_tools`. A simulation becomes its starting screen plus its states written out. Until then a day's Word documents differ from its website, and the status matrix says so.

## 9. Order of work for a day (the checklist)

1. Read the day's plan column and **write the map first**: UE row to block, with the number of questions; a row with no deliverable becomes a facilitator card. **On a full upgrade request ("upgrade Day N like Day 2") do not wait for a yes: write the map in the README and build (v1.1).** Only a decision this standard does not settle is asked.
2. Build each block from the plan's words (sections 1 and 2), one block at a time, slowly. **Audit the case for logic gaps (section 14) and close them in the same pass. Give every "item by several things" question a guided table (section 15).**
3. Pictures and simulations are built straight from the plan's clues (v1.1); show a mockup or demo in chat first only when the user asks to see it before it is built.
4. Saved state, missing list, mentor key and guides (EN and DE), answer key only for fixed picks, export, `verify:dayN`.
5. Sync the Materi when asked (section 6).
6. Check (section 7). Write the README "Notes on deviations", update this file, update the memory note.
7. Commit; push only when the user asks (pushing to `main` deploys to Vercel).
- **Engines:** Day 2 and Day 3 are hand-built (`components/dayN`); **Days 4 to 10 (and any later day built to this standard) use the free-form engine `dayFree`**: write `data/dayN/free.ts` with `defineSpec`, a kit `components/dayN/Free.tsx` with the pictures and simulations as slots, register the day in `components/day/dayConfigs.tsx`, `store/dayTypesStd.ts`, `store/useStore.ts` (mentor fill) and `scripts/verify-free.ts`; Days 11 to 16 still share `dayGen` (rebuild them on `dayFree` when asked, no question needed; the generic `dayGen` engine and its registry stay for them).

## 13. Story comics in pixel art (CLAUDE.md #54 points here)

Written 2026-10-17 from Days 2 to 6. A rule about the **website only** (Word keeps its own detail, #52, #53). Every study card carries one comic, a quiet row at the top of the card ("▶ Read the comic · title · teaser · n panels"), opening a dialog. **It replaces the card's "Walk me through it" story** (the `useStory` plan, the `<Story>` row, the spotlight classes and the "Press “Walk me through it”" sentence are removed; the sentence now says “Read the comic”). The diagram's own controls, "The point" and "What this shows" stay.

**Not a new story: the card's own material in pictures.** The people and the example company are the card's own (Day 2: Sofia, Ana, Lena, Tom, LearnLoop's Chief Product Officer; Day 3: Daniel, Mira, Priya; Day 4: Tom, Anna, Maya, Dana, Priya; Day 5: Mia, Priya, Dana, the finance director; Day 6: Mia, Sven, Petra, Priya, Dana, Sandra), the figures are the card's, and anything invented is labelled "Case assumption" in the comic's note. A card with no story (a static diagram) gets one told from its idea and its picture.

**Shape.** Four panels: the situation, the problem, the fix, and a last panel `THE RULE` that states the card's rule in one sentence and **names the task block that uses it** (a real block of the day: 1.1, 1.2, 2.1 or 3.1; there are no Optional blocks). A card that needs one more step has five (never more). Every panel has: the drawing, a scene line, speech (HTML), a tag that names what the panel shows (`tone` plain, teal or ink; the words carry the meaning, never the colour), an always-visible "What this panel means" (through `glossify`, #19) and optionally a "Look at:" line. Both languages are written by hand (#32).

**The style (approved by the user from a chat mockup).**
- A 160 by 90 grid in an SVG with `shapeRendering="crispEdges"`: chunky sprites with a **one-pixel outline in #1B1B1F**, flat colours, big heads, a face made from a few pixels, original characters (never someone else's). A pale-blue sky and a wooden floor (`Stage`), a table, a monitor, a board as props. No gradients, no shadows, no photo.
- **Speech is never drawn**: it is HTML beside the picture and wraps in both languages. Inside the drawing only numerals and short capital labels (`Txt`, a monospace face).

## 14. Gap audit of the case: close the logic holes without adding a figure (CLAUDE.md #61 points here)

Written 2026-10-17 from Day 2 and applied to Days 3 to 6. The plan states a day's case in a few lines. Participants with years of experience in digital learning read them critically and ask the facilitator what "limited", "unclear" or "a short time" mean, and the plan's Task 2 ("you are developing a new learning platform"), its case study (a named platform) and its Level 3 project (a role at an EdTech company) are three scenarios that the site presents as one case. A gap is **not** an error in the plan: a vague word is often the point (a decision must hold at any budget). The audit decides which words stay open on purpose, says so on the page, and settles the rest.

**The audit, once per day, before it ships (write the result under "Notes on deviations" in the README):**
1. **One company.** Every task names the same platform. The plan's "a new learning platform" becomes the case company's next version (a Case assumption), and a scenario about "a user" becomes a user of that platform.
2. **A condition says what it is for.** "Three months" is until the next management review, "four weeks" until the module goes live, "2 months" until the next review. No condition is left as a bare number of weeks.
3. **Every vague word is settled or listed as open.** Words such as limited, unclear, low, declining, growing, quickly, a short time, lasting, beginners get one of two treatments: a **qualitative Case assumption** (what exists, what is recorded, who says it, what the word means), or an entry under **open on purpose**. A figure, or an anchor that rules an option in or out ("not enough for a full build"), is never added: the answer must hold at any budget, and the learner says what they assume.
4. **Options say what is asked.** A question that asks to "choose" says what: the one you **start with**. A question that asks for an order says so; combining is what the order does.
5. **A "known and open" box in every case brief, Route 1 and Route 2** (`components/ui/CaseKnown.tsx`; in the free-form engine `brief1.known` and `brief2.known`): three columns, *In the brief* (the plan's own facts), *Clear · Case assumption*, *Open · on purpose*, and the sentence "As in most projects, not everything is known. Decide anyway, and say what you assume." The Case assumption callout says the facts marked "Clear" are made up.
6. **In the options block:** the platform's name, what the time is for, the sentence "As in most projects, the amount of the budget is not set … Decide anyway, and say what you assume.", and one step in the help of the justification field: "Say in one sentence what you assume about what is not known."
7. **A hidden facilitator card "Case questions · if a participant asks"** after each case brief (`FacilitatorCard` with `qa`; in the engine a card with `after: "brief1"` or `"brief2"`): the standard answers, so every group hears the same. It always holds the budget question (not set, on purpose; does the choice change at twice or half the budget), one on "which platform is this", one on "can I combine the options", one on "what data do we have" and one on "how many" (no figure is given, do not invent one). Nothing shows without the mentor passcode; it is not in the missing list, the ring, the page map or the export.
8. **Limits, always:** nothing contradicts the plan; nothing names the cause the model solution finds (the plan's Musterlösung); a Clear item is qualitative and consistent with the day's simulations (Day 2: what the desk table records); an Open item is a real question a test, an interview or a decision would answer.

**Checks.** `npm run verify:day4` to `day6` (the engine's checks: each brief has a known box with three columns of at least three points and one card of at least three answered questions); Days 2 and 3 are read in the browser. EN and DE: the box and the card in Route 1 and Route 2 of every day, no horizontal scroll. Reference: Day 2 (`CaseBrief` and `CaseQaCard` in `components/day2/Task1.tsx`, Route 2 in `Task2.tsx`), Day 3, and `data/day4/free.ts` for the engine.

**What each day settled (2026-10-17).** Day 2: LearnPro's next version, three months until the management review, "start with". Day 3: a module of EduCore, four weeks until it goes live, "beginners" means new to the subject. Day 4: the platform is MotivaLearn, two months until the review, "lasting" is judged after the two months. Day 5: the platform is LearnBase, growth so far means more registered learners, the stakeholders are management, marketing and the UX team. Day 6: the user of Block 1.2 is an EduPath learner, "the organisation" is the customer that licenses it, "after a short time" means after some lessons.

## 15. The guided table: a help for learners who do not know what to write (CLAUDE.md #62 points here)

Written 2026-10-17, first built on Day 2. Learners of this course are senior and critical and want the freedom of writing in their own words, so **the form stays the default**. A learner who is new to the topic, or who simply does not know how much to write or in which shape, can switch to a **guided table** with one tap on the quiet link under the field. The table is a help, never a requirement and never a different task. The idea comes from the Full Stack worksheets (a table with a worked row, named columns and a visible minimum tells a beginner what to fill in); what does not carry over is listed at the end.

**Where it applies.** Every question that asks for *several items, each described by several things* (a Level 3 question counts too: the data you need, a roadmap, the risks, a decision in three parts): the options of Task 2 against risk, benefit and effort (or impact, effort, risk, user value, business value), the differences between two platforms (what you compare, A, B), the UX tests (what, how, what you measure), the persona and journey phases (entry, usage, drop-out). Not for a plain list of items, a decision, a reason or a strategy: those stay a form.

**What the guided table has** (`FormOrTable` in `components/ui/FormOrTable.tsx` with `guide`, data in `data/dayN/tableGuides.ts`; Block 1.1 of Day 2 uses `GridTable` directly):
- it is **a real table, like the one in a Google Docs page**: borders, a shaded header row, a row header, and cells that are text fields without a box of their own. A cell grows with what is typed and the whole row grows with it; there is no inner scrollbar. The columns share the width; on a narrow screen the table scrolls inside its own frame, never the page. Rows that the plan names are the rows of the table (option A, B, C; test 1 to 3; five differences);
- **a hint under every column header**, in plain words ("what could go wrong or backfire");
- **one grey example row at the top, from another company or other platforms** (LearnLoop, two other platforms), never an answer to the case; a verify check keeps it out of the mentor key;
- **suggestions** for a scale column (`suggest`: Low, Medium, High as small taps under the cell that put "Low: " at the start of the cell, optional, replaceable by anything the learner types). A scale is a recommendation (section 2): the word alone does not count as an answer (`cellText` in `lib/day2/progress.ts`), the reason does;
- the link in the form reads "Not sure how to fill this in? Use the guided table." and in the table "Back to one box each." Both layouts are kept; only the chosen one counts (completeness, the missing list, the export).

**Tables whose rows the learner adds** (`components/ui/RowsTable.tsx`): where the plan does not say how many items (Day 2 Route 2: the data you need, a roadmap, the risks) the guided table starts with three rows and a quiet button adds one (up to eight) and removes an empty last row. A table answer counts from two complete rows (`MIN_ROWS`); a row with some cells and not all is named in the missing list, an empty row is ignored; a column that holds a short answer by design ("Have it already?", "Side") needs two characters, any other cell three. A roadmap table is numbered. A decision in fixed parts (what you decide, what you do not know, when you would reverse it) is a table with those rows and no add button. Saved rows survive a reload: the deep merge (`mergeDefaults`) keeps every saved array item, not only as many as the default has.

**Export and checks.** The export prints the layout the learner chose (a table as a table, a form as text); the guide, the example row and the hints are never exported, never checked and not in the mentor key. A cell with only a tapped scale word is named in the missing list. `verify:dayN` checks for each guide: a hint for every column, a full example row, the example is not part of the model answer, and a scale-only cell is still missing.

**Not taken over from the Full Stack worksheets:** a total score (/25) or any sum of ratings (the plan has no formula, #44); fixed dropdown values (the vocabulary is a suggestion); a worked row from the case itself (the example is always another company, #23); grade tiers Excellent, Fair and Poor on the page (the website does not grade; the assessment criteria are in the Trainer document, #53). A minimum count is shown as "what is asked", never as a grade.

**When a day gets it.** In the same pass as its tasks (section 9, step 2): list the "item by several things" questions of the day, write one `tableGuides.ts` (EN and DE, by hand), pass `guide` to the `FormOrTable` of each. **A day on the free-form engine** (`dayFree`, Days 4 to 6) writes the guides inside its one spec file: `table.guide` on a `lists` field, `tableGuide` on an `assess` field, and `table: { kind: "rows" | "frame", ... }` on a text field that can also be answered as a table (rows the learner adds, or fixed parts such as who decides, on what evidence, what happens next; the spec holds the model rows, the missing wording and the minimum cell). The engine does the rest (state `modes` and `rows`, missing list, export, mentor fill, `FrameTable` and `RowsTable`), and `verify-free` checks every guide. Which questions get a table on Days 4 to 6: the lists and the assessment, the evaluation, risk and architecture of Day 4, the needs, roadmap, conflict and architecture of Day 5, the personas and strategy of Day 6, and the decision under uncertainty on each; strategy, definition and other prose stay a form. **Day 3** (hand-built) has `data/day3/tableGuides.ts`, the problems and causes tables as `GridTable`, the assessment as `FormOrTable guide`, and in Route 2 four tables (three prioritised measures, the two sides of the risk, the decision logic and the decision in three parts, all as fixed rows with `FrameTable`). Reference: Day 2, then Days 3 to 6.
