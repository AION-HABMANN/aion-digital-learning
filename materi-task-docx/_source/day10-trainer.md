# Trainer guide

## How to use this document

- **Before the day:** read the lesson plan above and the model solution of the case study (UE5–6). Decide which Optional cards and blocks your group will skip.
- **During the day:** use the section of the unit you are in (UE4 coaching, UE6 debrief, UE7 feedback round, UE10 debrief). Show a model solution only after the participants have worked.
- **Reference answers:** they use the facts and the numbers printed in the Task document. Figures that the plan does not print are Case assumptions. Effort is rated by the printed cost rule taught in Materi A5; user impact and risk are judgements.
- **What is not here:** the Materi (the study material) and the Task (the worksheet) are separate documents and are handed out. This one is not.

## Assessment criteria (Bewertungskriterien)

Use these criteria to read what participants hand in and to give feedback in words. They are the same on every day; the reference answers below say what meets them on this day.

| Criterion | Level | Meets | Partly | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The user's side** | 1 | Describes and sorts from the learner's side; every statement points at a printed fact. | Mostly from the learner's side; some statements are about the design only, or no fact is named. | Describes the platform, not the learner; no fact named. |
| **Weighing under limits** | 2 | Rates user impact, effort and risk with a reason for each; effort follows the printed cost rule; budget and time are checked, or the overrun is explained. | Ratings given, reasons thin, or effort does not follow the rule. | Ratings missing or without reasons; the limits are ignored. |
| **Prioritising** | 2 | Gives a reason for position one (reach, cost or what the others depend on); the order fits the ratings. | An order is given; the reason covers the whole set or is generic. | No order, or no reason. |
| **Missing information** | 2 | Names something specific that would change the decision, and how to find it out. | Names a gap but not how to find it out. | “More data” with nothing specific. |
| **Conflict of goals** | 3 | Names the day's conflicting goals, says who or what loses, and justifies the order. | Names the conflict; the loser is not named. | Presents a plan in which everything wins. |
| **Decision under uncertainty** | 3 | Decides in one sentence, names an unknown, gives a reversal figure and a time, and says what is given up. | Decides; the reversal or the give-up is missing or vague. | Does not decide, or gives up nothing. |

**How to give feedback.** Say what is strong, name one criterion that is only partly met, and give one concrete next step. A choice that differs from the reference is not an error if the reason is clear and refers to a printed fact.

## Block 1.1 · model answers (UE2)

*Block 1.1: Eight facts: feedback, progress or phone? · Core · about 13 min.*

### Reference: Eight facts into Missing feedback, Unclear progress, Poor use on a phone

**Reference answer.** Missing feedback: facts 1, 2, 3 · Unclear progress: facts 4, 5, 6 · Poor use on a phone: facts 7, 8

| Item | Reference reading |
|---|---|
| 1 · Nothing changes when a task is finished | Missing feedback. The platform does not answer what the learner did: missing feedback. |
| 2 · A red cross and no reason | Missing feedback. The platform answers, but with a bare mark that explains nothing: missing feedback. |
| 3 · No message after saving | Missing feedback. No answer to an action, so the learner presses again to be sure: missing feedback. |
| 4 · No progress bar, no count of lessons | Unclear progress. The learner cannot see how far along they are: unclear progress. |
| 5 · Finished lessons look like unstarted ones | Unclear progress. The status of a lesson is not shown: unclear progress. |
| 6 · “Level 7” with no meaning | Unclear progress. A number that says nothing about what it measures is not transparent progress: unclear progress (and a risk of a misleading display). |
| 7 · Text cut off on a phone, sideways scrolling | Poor use on a phone. The layout was not adapted to the small screen: poor use on a phone. |
| 8 · A 20 px Next button at the page bottom | Poor use on a phone. A small target far from the thumb: poor use on a phone, and an accessibility problem too (target size). |

*Trainer note.* The sort is objective: each fact breaks one of the three areas of the day (feedback, progress, responsive design). Fact 8 touches accessibility too (target size), and fact 6 could be argued as feedback; the test is what the learner cannot do (hit the button; tell how far along they are). A learner who argues another placement with a reason defends the choice. The effect on motivation, the improvements and the reasons are judged. Plan: Arbeitsauftrag 1, describe 5 problems from the user's perspective, how this affects motivation, which improvements.

### Reference: The fact that would take motivation first

**Reference answer.** 1 · Nothing changes when a task is finished

| Option | In the reference answer? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · Nothing changes when a task is finished | Yes | Reference choice: effort with no sign that it counted is the quickest way to lose motivation. |
| 2 · A red cross and no reason | No | Defends: a mistake with no explanation feels like a verdict. |
| 3 · No message after saving | No | Defends: insecurity about whether work is safe. |
| 4 · No progress bar, no count of lessons | No | Defends: no sense of getting somewhere. |
| 5 · Finished lessons look like unstarted ones | No | Defends: finished work is invisible. |
| 6 · “Level 7” with no meaning | No | Defends: a number that cannot be interpreted. |
| 7 · Text cut off on a phone | No | Defends: most learners use a phone. |
| 8 · A 20 px Next button at the page bottom | No | Defends: friction at every lesson. |

*Trainer note.* Any of the eight defends. Look at the reason: it should say what the learner feels and what they stop doing. The plan's goal is to develop an understanding of feedback systems; the coaching says making progress visible raises motivation.

:::note 1.1 · How does this affect motivation?
**Model answer (an example).** Fact 1: a learner finishes a task and nothing changes on the screen. They put in effort and get no sign that it counted, so they feel unseen, wonder if it was worth it and, after a few tasks, stop.

**Why.** Any of the eight facts defends if the reason says what the learner feels and what they stop doing (Materi A1, A2: feedback and progress as a tool for motivation).

**Look for.**
- Says what the learner feels.
- Says what they stop doing.
- Refers to a fact by number.
:::

:::note 1.1 · Which improvements do you suggest?
**Model answer (an example).** 1. Show a message and a tick when a task is finished, and say what it counted towards (facts 1 and 3). 2. Add a progress bar and a status for every lesson: not started, in progress, done (facts 4 and 5). 3. Adapt the layout to a phone: text that fits and a Next button at least 44 px high within thumb reach (facts 7 and 8).

**Why.** The plan asks which improvements you suggest. Each should name the fact it answers and answer one of the three problems (Materi A1 to A3).

**Look for.**
- Names the fact each improvement answers.
- Starts each with a verb.
- Answers all three problems, or says why not.
:::

*Block 1.2: Prioritising UX systems: three options · Optional · about 8 min.*

### Reference: Prioritising UX systems: three options

**Reference answer.** Reference order: B (Optimise the platform for mobile), then A (Introduce a progress indicator), then C (Develop a detailed feedback system)

| Option | In the reference answer? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A · Introduce a progress indicator (€7,000, 3 weeks) | Yes | It answers how far along am I at a low price, but only helps if it also works on a phone. Reference ratings: Impact Mid, effort Low (by the printed cost), risk Low; position 2. |
| B · Optimise the platform for mobile (€16,000, 5 weeks) | Yes | Most users are on a phone, so everything else has to work there first: responsive design is a prerequisite. Reference ratings: Impact High, effort Mid (by the printed cost), risk Mid; position 1. |
| C · Develop a detailed feedback system (€24,000, 7 weeks) | Yes | It is valuable, but costs the most and risks overloading learners; it comes after the basics. Reference ratings: Impact Mid, effort High (by the printed cost), risk High; position 3. |

*Trainer note.* The plan's goal is to make systemic UX decisions.

:::note 1.2 · Justify your decision: make a systemic UX decision
**Model answer (an example).** I put B first: most of our users are on a phone, so a progress indicator or feedback that does not work there reaches nobody; responsive design is a prerequisite. A comes second: it is the cheapest way to show learners how far along they are. C comes last: detailed feedback costs the most and risks overloading learners before the basics work.

**Why.** The plan's coaching: responsive design is not an option but a prerequisite. A first also defends if the reason says that transparency is the cheapest quick win and what it leaves open.

**Look for.**
- Names what each option does for phone users.
- Says what it leaves open.
:::

# UE4 · Coaching: transition from Level 1 to Level 2

## Coaching Focus: UX as a Steering and Feedback System (UE4)

**The plan's coaching points.**

- Users need orientation and feedback
- Making progress visible = increasing motivation
- Responsive design is not an option, but a prerequisite
- Accessibility = scalability + reach

**Reflection questions.**

- Where does the user lack orientation?
- Which information is decisive for motivation?
- Which UX decision works on all devices?

**How to run the unit (about 45 minutes).**

| Step | Minutes | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 5 | Say the coaching focus in one sentence: “UX as a Steering and Feedback System.” Say that in the next unit they will not describe, they will decide. |
| 2 | 10 | Pairs talk through the reflection questions; the notes go into Block 1.3 (never scored). |
| 3 | 15 | Collect two or three answers. Link each to a fact or a finding from Block 1.1, so that the answer is about this platform and not about UX in general. |
| 4 | 10 | Bridge: Level 1 was reading the platform from the user's side. Level 2 asks you to rate and order measures under a budget and a deadline, and to say what information you are missing. Show the first step of Block 2.2. |
| 5 | 5 | Open questions; set the briefing for UE5. |

# UE7 · Feedback round

## Feedback with a Strategic Perspective (UE7)

**Preparation (from the plan).**

- Presentation of the solutions

**Level 3 input: ask these questions.**

- Which measure improves user retention the most?
- Where is feedback underestimated?
- Which decision has the greatest scaling effect?

**Discussion.**

- Feedback vs. information overload
- Mobile UX as a standard

**How to run the unit (about 45 minutes).**

| Step | Minutes | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 20 | Each group presents for about five minutes. The focus is the reasoning, not the result: why this first priority, what was left out. |
| 2 | 15 | Put the Level 3 input questions to the whole room, one at a time. Ask for the answer in business terms: what it moves (completion, retention, engagement, cost). |
| 3 | 10 | Run the discussion. Close with one sentence: the same measures, ordered under a different goal, give a different plan. That is the step to Level 3. |

## Transition from Level 2 to Level 3 (Übergang)

Level 2 asks a participant to decide among measures. Level 3 asks them to decide as a manager, between goals that pull apart. The day's conflict is: **Conflicting goals: information density against clarity, feedback against overload, flexibility against consistency** (Materi B2).

**A participant is ready for Level 3 (Route 2, Task 2) when:**

1. the Core of Task 1 is done and the reason for position one names a reach, a cost or a dependency;
2. the missing information is specific and comes with a way to find it out;
3. they can say the day's conflict in one sentence and name what loses when each side wins.

**If a participant is not there yet:**

- Ask for the reason of position one again, using the “How to decide” box of Materi A5.
- Pair them for UE8 with someone who is ready, for the brief and the first block.
- Give them the “In plain words” box of Materi B1 before the brief; Route 2 does not need Route 1 answers.

Route 2 does not require Route 1: a participant may start the transfer project without having finished Task 1, and use their own answers in the memo if they have them.

## Block 3.1 · model answers (UE9)

*Block 3.1: Feedback, progress tracking, devices and accessibility · Core · about 10 min.*

### Reference: The concept for feedback

**Reference answer.** An explained result after every task, with a next step; A message and a tick when a task is saved or finished; A short note at a milestone: half the course done; An error hint that says what to change

| Option | In the reference answer? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| An explained result after every task, with a next step | Yes | Direct feedback that explains and leads on. |
| A message and a tick when a task is saved or finished | Yes | Confirms that the work counted. |
| A short note at a milestone: half the course done | Yes | Indirect feedback: visible progress at the moments that matter. |
| An error hint that says what to change | Yes | Turns a mistake into a next step. |
| A pop-up after every single click | No | Rejected: feedback that overloads and interrupts. |
| A badge for every login | No | Rejected: rewards activity, not progress. |
| Feedback only at the end of the course | No | Rejected: too late to steer anything. |
| A red or green mark with no words | No | Rejected: a bare mark that explains nothing and fails for colour-blind learners. |

*Trainer note.* At least three; any three of the four reference elements defend if together they say that the work counted, why, and what next. The rejected ones overload, reward activity or come too late. Plan: item 1, a concept for feedback systems.

### Reference: The strategy for progress tracking

**Reference answer.** A progress bar for each course, based on lessons finished; A status for every lesson: not started, in progress, done; A “Continue where you stopped” entry; An estimate of the time left in the course

| Option | In the reference answer? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A progress bar for each course, based on lessons finished | Yes | Transparent and based on something real. |
| A status for every lesson: not started, in progress, done | Yes | Makes finished work visible. |
| A “Continue where you stopped” entry | Yes | Turns progress into the next step. |
| An estimate of the time left in the course | Yes | Helps a learner decide whether to start now. |
| One overall score that hides the details | No | Rejected: not transparent; the learner cannot tell what it means. |
| A level number that does not say what it measures | No | Rejected: a misleading display (the plan's risk). |
| A ranking against all other learners | No | Rejected: demotivates the many who are not at the top. |
| A bar that fills fast at the start and slowly at the end | No | Rejected: a misleading display that feels like a cheat later. |

*Trainer note.* At least three; any three of the four reference elements defend if the display is honest and based on something real. The rejected ones mislead (an unexplained level, a bar that cheats) or demotivate (a ranking). Plan: item 2, a strategy for progress tracking.

### Reference: Content by device

**Reference answer.** Same on every device: items 2, 3, 4 · First on a phone: items 1, 5 · Only on larger screens: items 6, 7 · Not shown on a phone: items 8

| Item | Reference reading |
|---|---|
| 1 · “Continue where you stopped” | First on a phone. The most common phone use is a few minutes on the next lesson. |
| 2 · The progress bar and the lesson status | Same on every device. Transparency must not depend on the device. |
| 3 · The lesson text | Same on every device. The core content on every device, with a layout that fits. |
| 4 · A video with captions | Same on every device. Open item: on a phone it may come after the text; captions on every device. |
| 5 · A short quiz question after a section | First on a phone. Open item: same on every device also defends; short questions suit phones. |
| 6 · Notes next to the lesson, side by side | Only on larger screens. Needs width; on a phone notes open as a separate layer. |
| 7 · The full course catalogue with filters | Only on larger screens. Browsing many courses suits a larger screen; on a phone a shorter list with search. |
| 8 · A banner for current offers | Not shown on a phone. On a small screen it pushes the lesson down and competes with the main action. |

*Trainer note.* There is no single right grid. Items 4 and 5 are the open ones; any placement with a reason from the learner's use defends. Each bin must be used at least once. Plan: item 3, a responsive design concept (content prioritised by device).

### Reference: The accessibility integration

:::note 3.1 · In two sentences: how do feedback, progress and a phone-first layout work together so that a learner always knows how they are doing?
