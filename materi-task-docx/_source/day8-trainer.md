# Trainer guide

## How to use this document

- **Before the day:** read the lesson plan above and the model solution of the case study (UE5–6). Decide which Optional cards and blocks your group will skip.
- **During the day:** use the section of the unit you are in (UE4 coaching, UE6 debrief, UE7 feedback round, UE10 debrief). Show a model solution only after the participants have worked.
- **Reference answers:** they use the facts and the numbers printed in the Task document. Figures that the plan does not print are Case assumptions. Effort is rated by the printed cost rule taught in Materi A5; user impact and risk are judgements.
- **What is not here:** the Materi (the study material) and the Task (the worksheet) are separate documents and are handed out. This one is not.

## Assessment criteria (Bewertungskriterien)

Use these criteria to read what participants hand in and to give feedback in words. They are the same on every day; the reference answers below say what meets them on this day.

| Criterion | Level | Meets | Partly | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The user's side** | 1 | Describes and sorts from the learner's side; every statement points at a printed fact. | Mostly from the learner's side; some statements are about the design only, or no fact is named. | Describes the platform, not the learner; no fact named. |
| **Weighing under limits** | 2 | Rates user impact, effort and risk with a reason for each; effort follows the printed cost rule; budget and time are checked, or the overrun is explained. | Ratings given, reasons thin, or effort does not follow the rule. | Ratings missing or without reasons; the limits are ignored. |
| **Prioritising** | 2 | Gives a reason for position one (reach, cost or what the others depend on); the order fits the ratings. | An order is given; the reason covers the whole set or is generic. | No order, or no reason. |
| **Missing information** | 2 | Names something specific that would change the decision, and how to find it out. | Names a gap but not how to find it out. | “More data” with nothing specific. |
| **Conflict of goals** | 3 | Names the day's conflicting goals, says who or what loses, and justifies the order. | Names the conflict; the loser is not named. | Presents a plan in which everything wins. |
| **Decision under uncertainty** | 3 | Decides in one sentence, names an unknown, gives a reversal figure and a time, and says what is given up. | Decides; the reversal or the give-up is missing or vague. | Does not decide, or gives up nothing. |

**How to give feedback.** Say what is strong, name one criterion that is only partly met, and give one concrete next step. A choice that differs from the reference is not an error if the reason is clear and refers to a printed fact.

# UE2–3 · Level 1 tasks

## Block 1.1 · model answers (UE2)

*Block 1.1: Eight facts: navigation, overload or inconsistency? · Core · about 13 min.*

### Reference: Eight facts into Unclear navigation, Visual overload, Inconsistent design

**Reference answer.** Unclear navigation: facts 1, 2, 3 · Visual overload: facts 4, 5 · Inconsistent design: facts 6, 7, 8

| Item | Reference reading |
|---|---|
| 1 · Four menu levels; one course under three entries | Unclear navigation. The same item has several homes; the learner cannot tell where it is. |
| 2 · The menu marks “Home” inside a course | Unclear navigation. The marked entry and the page disagree, so the learner cannot tell where they are. |
| 3 · No “Next” link at the end of a lesson | Unclear navigation. The learner does not know what comes next. |
| 4 · Nine colours, four sizes, three banners | Visual overload. Many elements compete and nothing leads. |
| 5 · Five equal buttons in a row | Visual overload. The plan's “several buttons without priority”: there is no main thing. |
| 6 · Links in three different colours | Inconsistent design. The same function looks different in different places. |
| 7 · “Submit” looks different on three screens | Inconsistent design. |
| 8 · “Back” does different things on different screens | Inconsistent design: the same function works differently. |

*Trainer note.* The sort is objective: each fact breaks one of the three mistakes the plan names. Fact 8 touches navigation too; the test is the phrase printed: the same control behaving differently is inconsistency. A learner who argues another placement with a reason defends the choice.

:::note 1.1 · Where confusion arises
**Model answer (an example).** Fact 3: at the end of a lesson there is no “Next” link. A beginner who has just finished a lesson does not know what to do and has to go back up three levels; many will stop there.

**Why.** Any of the eight facts defends if the reason says what the learner wants to do at that point and what they cannot tell.

**Look for.**
- Says what the learner wants to do.
- Says what they cannot tell.
- Refers to a fact by number.
:::

:::note 1.1 · What you would improve intuitively
**Model answer (an example).** Add a “Next lesson” button at the end of every lesson (fact 3). Mark the real place in the menu (fact 2). Make one button the main button and turn the others into quiet links (fact 5).

**Why.** The plan asks for intuitive improvements; each should name the fact it answers.

**Look for.**
- Names the fact each improvement answers.
- Improves structure before decoration.
:::

## Block 1.2 · model answers (UE3)

*Block 1.2: Making design decisions: three options under three weeks · Optional · about 8 min.*

### Reference: Three design options under three weeks

**Reference answer.** Reference order: B (restructure the navigation), then A (simplify visually), then C (add visual elements)

| Option | In the reference answer? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A · Simplify the design visually (€9,000, 2 weeks) | Yes | Reference ratings: user impact Mid (it calms the page but does not fix the menu), effort Mid (€9,000 is within €8,000 to €15,000), risk Low. |
| B · Restructure the navigation (€14,000, 3 weeks) | Yes | Reference ratings: user impact High (the learner can find, choose and carry on), effort Mid, risk Mid (it needs all three weeks). |
| C · Add more visual elements (€7,000, 2 weeks) | No | Reference ratings: user impact Low (it adds things to look at, it helps nobody find anything), effort Low (under €8,000), risk High (more clutter). Rejected. |

*Trainer note.* Effort follows the printed cost by the rule in Materi A5 (under €8,000 Low, €8,000 to €15,000 Mid, above €15,000 High). The plan's goal is to weigh structure against design: structure beats visual design.

:::note 1.2 · Justify your decision
**Model answer (an example).** I put B first: beginners are lost because they cannot find content and do not know what comes next, and only the navigation answers that. A comes second: a calmer page helps, but a calm page with the same course under three entries still loses them. C comes last: it only changes how the platform looks.

**Look for.**
- Says which option fixes what the learner cannot do and which only changes the look.
:::

# UE4 · Coaching: transition from Level 1 to Level 2

## Coaching Focus: Design Is Decision Architecture (UE4)

**The plan's coaching points.**

- UI is not about "making it pretty", but about "making it understandable"
- Structure beats visual design
- Good UX reduces the user's wrong decisions
- Connection: design → behavior → learning success

**Reflection questions.**

- What really guides the user?
- Where does uncertainty arise in the interface?
- Which design decision has the greatest effect?

**How to run the unit (about 45 minutes).**

| Step | Minutes | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 5 | Say the coaching focus in one sentence: “Design Is Decision Architecture.” Say that in the next unit they will not describe, they will decide. |
| 2 | 10 | Pairs talk through the reflection questions; the notes go into Block 1.3 (never scored). |
| 3 | 15 | Collect two or three answers. Link each to a fact or a finding from Block 1.1, so that the answer is about this platform and not about UX in general. |
| 4 | 10 | Bridge: Level 1 was reading the platform from the user's side. Level 2 asks you to rate and order measures under a budget and a deadline, and to say what information you are missing. Show the first step of Block 2.2. |
| 5 | 5 | Open questions; set the briefing for UE5. |

## Block 1.3 · model answers (UE4)

:::note 1.3 · Coaching reflection (never scored)
**Model answer (an example).** What guides the user: a path, a marked place and one main button. Uncertainty arises where the menu and the page disagree and where there is no next step. The decision with the greatest effect: giving every item one home and every lesson a next step.

**Why.** Reflection is never scored. The plan's coaching points: UI is not about making it pretty but about making it understandable; structure beats visual design; good UX reduces the user's wrong decisions. Use the notes as the start of the live discussion.
:::

# UE5–6 · Case study (2 UE)

## The plan's model solution: core logic (UE5–6)

Show this only after the participants have worked, at the end of UE6.

- **Main problem:** lack of structure
- **Measures (as the plan lists them):**
  - clear information architecture
  - Reduction of visual complexity
  - unambiguous navigation
  - clear action guidance

**Rationale (plan):** Structure → orientation → usage

## Block 2.1 · model answers (UE5–6)

*Block 2.1: Four structural problems · Optional · about 7 min.*

### Reference: Four structural problems

**Reference answer.** Content cannot be found (one item in several places) · no clear visual hierarchy · navigation not unambiguous · no clear guidance for action

| Option | In the reference answer? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Learners cannot find content: one item sits in several places under different names | Yes | Fact 1 and the case: users cannot find content. |
| There is no clear visual hierarchy: everything competes for attention | Yes | Facts 4 and 5; the case names a lack of hierarchy. |
| The navigation is not unambiguous: learners cannot tell where they are | Yes | Facts 2 and 3; the case names no clear navigation. |
| There is no clear guidance for action: no main button and no next step | Yes | Facts 3 and 5. |
| The courses are too long | No | No printed fact says so. |
| The subscription price is too high | No | A business matter; nothing printed. |
| There are too few courses | No | A content matter; the case is about structure. |

*Trainer note.* A structural problem is defended by a printed fact. The plan's model solution names the main problem as a lack of structure.

:::note 2.1 · Which printed fact supports your first problem
**Model answer (an example).** Fact 1: the same course can be reached under “Learn”, “Library” and “Archive”, so learners do not know which entry is the real one.

**Look for.**
- Points at a fact by number or quotes the case.
:::

## Block 2.2 · model answers (UE5–6)

*Block 2.2: Four improvements, rated, ordered and justified · Core · about 17 min.*

### Reference: Four improvements, rated and ordered

**Reference answer.** M1 clear information architecture · M2 reduce visual complexity · M3 unambiguous navigation · M4 clear action guidance. Total €41,000 against €40,000; longest measure 5 weeks of 10.

| Option | In the reference answer? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| M1 · A clear information architecture (€16,000, 5 weeks) | Yes | Effort High by the printed cost (above €15,000). Reference ratings: user impact High (one place per item), risk Mid (a large change). |
| M2 · Reduce visual complexity (€8,000, 3 weeks) | Yes | Effort Mid (€8,000 is the lower edge of Mid). Reference ratings: user impact Mid, risk Low. |
| M3 · Unambiguous navigation (€11,000, 4 weeks) | Yes | Effort Mid. Reference ratings: user impact High (where am I, what comes next), risk Low. |
| M4 · A clear action guidance (€6,000, 2 weeks) | Yes | Effort Low. Reference ratings: user impact High for its cost (one main button), risk Low. |
| M5 · Consistent components (€13,000, 5 weeks) | No | Defends: answers facts 6, 7 and 8. Effort Mid. It is a second wave: consistency matters once the structure is fixed. |
| M6 · More visual elements (€18,000, 6 weeks) | No | Rejected: adds decoration; effort High, risk High (more clutter). |
| M7 · A dark mode (€10,000, 4 weeks) | No | Rejected: the look; no printed fact asks for it. |
| M8 · A new logo and brand name (€22,000, 6 weeks) | No | Rejected: the brand; learners who are lost need a structure, not a new name. |
| M9 · A search with AI suggestions (€30,000, 10 weeks) | No | Rejected: adds technology on top of a structure that is not clear; effort High. |

*Trainer note.* The plan's model solution: clear information architecture, reduction of visual complexity, unambiguous navigation, clear action guidance; rationale “Structure → orientation → usage”. The reference set costs €41,000, €1,000 over the printed budget by plan fidelity; the worksheet allows going over with a reason. A set within budget is M3, M4, M2 and M5 (€38,000), but it leaves the architecture unchanged, and “structure beats visual design”: whoever picks it should say why. Reference order: M1, M3, M2, M4; M3 first also defends (a quick win in four weeks).

:::note 2.2 · Why the first priority goes first
**Model answer (an example).** M1 goes first: structure leads to orientation and orientation leads to use. As long as the same course sits in three places, a calmer page or a better button only decorates a menu the beginner cannot read.

**Why.** The worksheet asks for the chain structure, orientation, use.

**Look for.**
- Uses the chain structure → orientation → use.
- Gives a reason for position one, not for the whole set.
:::

:::note 2.2 · What information you are missing
**Model answer (an example).** I do not know where beginners actually get lost: the facts say what is there, not which step makes them stop. I would watch five beginners find one course in a tree test before I rebuild the architecture.

**Why.** The plan's wording is “what information is missing”. A good answer is specific and says how to find it out.

**Look for.**
- Names something specific.
- Says how to find it out.
:::

## Debrief of the case study (UE6)

| Step | Minutes | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 15 | Groups of three or four compare their four measures and their order. They name where they differ and why. |
| 2 | 15 | Each group gives the reason for its first priority in one sentence. Write the reasons on a board; do not write the measures. |
| 3 | 10 | Show the plan's core logic and the rationale line (“Structure → orientation → usage”). Ask: where does a different order still defend? |
| 4 | 5 | Ask each participant to write one thing they would now rate differently. |

# UE7 · Feedback round

## Feedback with a Strategic Perspective (UE7)

**Preparation (from the plan).**

- Presentation of the solutions

**Level 3 input: ask these questions.**

- Which measure improves orientation the most?
- Where is design overestimated?
- Which decision reduces the drop-out rate sustainably?

**Discussion.**

- Design vs. functionality
- UX as steering of behavior

**How to run the unit (about 45 minutes).**

| Step | Minutes | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 20 | Each group presents for about five minutes. The focus is the reasoning, not the result: why this first priority, what was left out. |
| 2 | 15 | Put the Level 3 input questions to the whole room, one at a time. Ask for the answer in business terms: what it moves (completion, retention, engagement, cost). |
| 3 | 10 | Run the discussion. Close with one sentence: the same measures, ordered under a different goal, give a different plan. That is the step to Level 3. |

## Transition from Level 2 to Level 3 (Übergang)

Level 2 asks a participant to decide among measures. Level 3 asks them to decide as a manager, between goals that pull apart. The day's conflict is: **Conflicting goals: design against functionality, and too complex against too reduced** (Materi B2).

**A participant is ready for Level 3 (Route 2, Task 2) when:**

1. the Core of Task 1 is done and the reason for position one names a reach, a cost or a dependency;
2. the missing information is specific and comes with a way to find it out;
3. they can say the day's conflict in one sentence and name what loses when each side wins.

**If a participant is not there yet:**

- Ask for the reason of position one again, using the “How to decide” box of Materi A5.
- Pair them for UE8 with someone who is ready, for the brief and the first block.
- Give them the “In plain words” box of Materi B1 before the brief; Route 2 does not need Route 1 answers.

Route 2 does not require Route 1: a participant may start the transfer project without having finished Task 1, and use their own answers in the memo if they have them.

## Block 3.1 · model answers (UE9)

*Block 3.1: Basic structure, principles for visual design and user guidance · Core · about 10 min.*

### Reference: The basic structure: a closed card sort of twelve items

**Reference answer.** A Learn: 1, 6, 12 · B Find: 2, 3 · C Progress: 4, 5 · D Help and account: 7, 8, 9 · E Trainer area: 10, 11

| Item | Reference reading |
|---|---|
| 1 · Continue where I stopped | A Learn. It is the learner's way back into the course. |
| 2 · Course catalogue | B Find. |
| 3 · Search | B Find. |
| 4 · My certificates | C Progress. A certificate is the result of progress. |
| 5 · Progress in each course | C Progress. |
| 6 · My notes | A Learn. Open item: C or D also defend; notes belong to the lesson the learner is in. |
| 7 · Reminder settings | D Help and account. Open item: it also supports progress. |
| 8 · Help centre | D Help and account. |
| 9 · Profile and password | D Help and account. |
| 10 · Create a course (for trainers) | E Trainer area. |
| 11 · Learner list of my course (for trainers) | E Trainer area. |
| 12 · Messages from my trainer | A Learn (the learner's side). Open item: E or D defend; decide by where the learner looks for it. |

*Trainer note.* A card sort has no single right answer. Items 6, 7 and 12 are the open ones; any placement with a reason from the learner's side defends. Each section must be used at least once.

### Reference: Principles for visual design and the concept for user guidance

**Reference answer.** Principles (at least three): one main action per screen · at most three colours and one accent · the same function always looks the same · text contrast at least 4.5 : 1, no information by colour alone · group with space and proximity. Guidance (at least three): a path and a marked menu entry · a “Next” button · a “Continue where you stopped” entry · progress on each course page · plain labels.

| Option | In the reference answer? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| One main action per screen | Yes | Answers fact 5. |
| At most three colours and one accent colour | Yes | Answers fact 4. |
| The same function always looks the same (a style guide) | Yes | Answers facts 6 to 8. |
| Text contrast of at least 4.5 : 1 and no information by colour alone | Yes | A rule that can be checked. |
| Group with space and proximity, not with boxes everywhere | Yes | A hierarchy rule. |
| New courses may choose their own colours for variety | No | Rejected: it creates the inconsistency of facts 6 to 8. |
| Add animation to keep learners interested | No | Rejected: decoration; the case already has three animated banners. |
| Every screen shows a banner for current offers | No | Rejected: competes with the main action. |
| Guidance · A path above the page and a marked menu entry | Yes | Answers facts 1 and 2. |
| Guidance · A “Next” or “Continue” button after every lesson | Yes | Answers fact 3. |
| Guidance · A “Continue where you stopped” entry on the home screen | Yes | Orientation on the first screen. |
| Guidance · Progress shown on each course page | Yes | Tells the learner how far they are. |
| Guidance · Plain labels named after what the learner does | Yes | Names sections by what the learner does. |
| Guidance · A pop-up tour on every page | No | Rejected: interrupts and adds noise. |
| Guidance · Tooltips on every element | No | Rejected: explains a structure that should explain itself. |

*Trainer note.* A principle is good if it can be used to say no to a proposal. Ask the learner which proposal each principle would reject.

:::note 3.1 · How a learner always knows where they are and what comes next
**Model answer (an example).** A path above the page and the marked menu entry say where they are; a “Next” button after each lesson and “Continue where you stopped” on the home screen say what comes next.

**Look for.**
- Answers both questions: where am I, what comes next.
- Uses at least two of the guidance elements chosen.
:::

## Block 3.2 · model answers (UE10)

*Block 3.2: Measures, risk, a decision under uncertainty, and what you give up · Core · about 10 min.*

### Reference: Three prioritised design measures

**Reference answer.** D1 rebuild the information architecture and navigation · D3 redesign home and course pages around one main action · D4 tests of the new structure with learners. €135,000 of €210,000; longest 20 weeks.

| Option | In the reference answer? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| D1 · Rebuild the information architecture and navigation (€70,000, 20 weeks) | Yes | Structure first: the cause of the lost orientation. |
| D2 · A design system with a style guide (€50,000, 24 weeks) | No | Defends as the second wave: consistency after structure. In place of D4 if the reason says why tests can wait. |
| D3 · Redesign the home and course pages around one main action (€40,000, 14 weeks) | Yes | Hierarchy and guidance on the pages learners meet first. |
| D4 · Tests of the new structure with learners (€25,000, 10 weeks) | Yes | The structure is decided without user tests; D4 is how it is checked later. |
| D5 · A guided first visit for beginners (€20,000, 8 weeks) | No | Defends, but guiding learners through a confusing structure treats the symptom; it follows D1. |
| D6 · A full visual rebrand (€60,000, 20 weeks) | No | Rejected: the look before the structure; one of the risks the worksheet names. |
| D7 · Animated elements everywhere (€45,000, 16 weeks) | No | Rejected: decoration; more things competing for attention. |

*Trainer note.* Reference order: D1, D3, D4. D1 first because structure leads to orientation and orientation to use. D4 third, run next to the build, so that the decision made without tests is checked early. D1, D2, D3 also defends.

### Reference: The biggest risk: too complex against too reduced

**Reference answer.** Reference: “We removed so much that learners and trainers lost help and functions they needed.” Any of the five defends with a reason and an action.

| Item | Reference reading |
|---|---|
| The new structure is still too complex, and learners are still lost | Action: a tree test before release; notice it in the first weeks by support questions. |
| We removed so much that learners and trainers lost help and functions they needed | The too-reduced failure. Action: test with trainers as well as learners; notice it in support requests in the first four weeks. |
| The new section names did not make sense to beginners | Action: name sections after what the learner does and test the names. |
| The style guide was ignored and the platform drifted back to inconsistency | Action: components in the design system and a review gate. |
| The budget was spent on the look before the structure was fixed | Action: order the work: structure first. |

*Trainer note.* The learner should say when they would notice the risk, not only what they would do.

:::note 3.2 · Why the first measure goes first
**Model answer (an example).** D1 goes first: the structure is the reason learners lose orientation, and with it fixed every later measure (hierarchy, guidance, tests) has something solid to work on.

**Look for.**
- Says what it does for orientation.
- Says what it does for the learner's behaviour.
:::

:::note 3.2 · What you do about the risk
**Model answer (an example).** I run a tree test with learners and trainers before the release and watch the support requests in the first four weeks; if trainers cannot find their functions, I restore them in the trainer area at once.

**Look for.**
- One action.
- Says when the risk would be noticed.
:::

:::note 3.2 · The decision under uncertainty (without user tests)
**Model answer (an example).** I decide to rebuild the information architecture into five sections named after what the learner does (D1) before any visual redesign. I do not know how beginners will group the twelve items. I reverse if, by the end of week 8, fewer than 80% of 20 tested learners find a given course in under a minute in a tree test.

**Why.** Four parts: what you decide, what you do not know, what would make you reverse it (a figure) and by when, and what you give up.

**Look for.**
- States the decision in one sentence.
- Names a specific unknown.
- Gives a reversal condition with a figure and a time.
:::

:::note 3.2 · What you give up or postpone
**Model answer (an example).** I give up the full rebrand (D6) and the animated elements (D7) this year; marketing will miss the new look.

**Why.** A plan that gives up nothing has not decided.

**Look for.**
- Names something real that someone would miss.
:::

## Debrief of the transfer project (UE10)

| Step | Minutes | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 25 | Each participant presents the decision under uncertainty in the four parts: what I decide, what I do not know, when I reverse (a figure and a time), what I give up. Two minutes each; the others ask one question. |
| 2 | 10 | Name the losers: what did each plan give up, and who would miss it? A plan with no loser has not decided. |
| 3 | 10 | Show the model solution of Block 3.1 and 3.2 and the plan's goals: think like a decision-maker, understand the day as a system, take responsibility. Compare, do not correct. |
