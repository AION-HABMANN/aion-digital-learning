# Trainer guide

## How to use this document

- **Before the day:** read the lesson plan above and the model solution of the case study (UE5–6). Decide which Optional cards and blocks your group will skip.
- **During the day:** use the section of the unit you are in (UE4 coaching, UE6 debrief, UE7 feedback round, UE10 debrief). Show a model solution only after the participants have worked.
- **Reference answers:** they use the facts and the numbers printed in the Task document. Figures that the plan does not print are Case assumptions. Effort is rated by the printed cost rule taught in Materi A5; user impact and risk are judgements.
- **What is not here:** the Materi (the study material) and the Task (the worksheet) are separate documents and are handed out. This one is not.

## Assessment criteria (Bewertungskriterien)

Use these criteria to read what participants hand in and to give feedback in words. They are the same on every day; the reference answers below say what meets them on this day.

| Criterion | Level | Meets | Partly | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The user's side** | 1 | Describes and sorts from the learner's side; every statement points at a printed fact. | Mostly from the learner's side; some statements are about the design only, or no fact is named. | Describes the platform, not the learner; no fact named. |
| **Weighing under limits** | 2 | Rates user impact, effort and risk with a reason for each; effort follows the printed cost rule; budget and time are checked, or the overrun is explained. | Ratings given, reasons thin, or effort does not follow the rule. | Ratings missing or without reasons; the limits are ignored. |
| **Prioritising** | 2 | Gives a reason for position one (reach, cost or what the others depend on); the order fits the ratings. | An order is given; the reason covers the whole set or is generic. | No order, or no reason. |
| **Missing information** | 2 | Names something specific that would change the decision, and how to find it out. | Names a gap but not how to find it out. | “More data” with nothing specific. |
| **Conflict of goals** | 3 | Names the day's conflicting goals, says who or what loses, and justifies the order. | Names the conflict; the loser is not named. | Presents a plan in which everything wins. |
| **Decision under uncertainty** | 3 | Decides in one sentence, names an unknown, gives a reversal figure and a time, and says what is given up. | Decides; the reversal or the give-up is missing or vague. | Does not decide, or gives up nothing. |

**How to give feedback.** Say what is strong, name one criterion that is only partly met, and give one concrete next step. A choice that differs from the reference is not an error if the reason is clear and refers to a printed fact.

# UE2–3 · Level 1 tasks

## Block 1.1 · model answers (UE2)

*Block 1.1: Eight problems: which kind, which first, and what belongs on a phone · Core · about 13 min.*

### Reference: Eight problems into Length, Adaptation, Continuity

**Reference answer.** Length: facts 1, 2, 7 · Adaptation: facts 3, 4, 8 · Continuity: facts 5, 6

| Item | Reference reading |
|---|---|
| 1 · One 40-minute video, no pause points | Length. It cannot be used in a few minutes: a length problem. |
| 2 · An 1,800-word page; the phone shows 150 at a time | Length. A text far too long for one sitting: a length problem. |
| 3 · A shrunk desktop menu with links 16 px apart | Adaptation. The layout was not made for a thumb: an adaptation problem. |
| 4 · A nine-column table that scrolls sideways | Adaptation. Content was not reduced for a small screen: an adaptation problem. |
| 5 · Phone progress is missing on the laptop | Continuity. Progress is not synchronised: a continuity problem. |
| 6 · On the laptop the learner searches again from the home page | Continuity. The place is lost between devices: a continuity problem. |
| 7 · A twelve-question quiz that loses all answers on leaving | Length. It demands one long sitting: a length problem. |
| 8 · Videos with no captions, sound needed | Adaptation. It ignores the setting where phones are used: an adaptation problem. |

*Trainer note.* The sort is objective in its core: a 40-minute video, an 1,800-word page and a twelve-question quiz that must be finished in one go are length; a shrunk menu, a nine-column table and videos with no captions are adaptation; progress that does not sync and a lost place are continuity. Fact 7 could also be argued as continuity because answers are lost; the test is whether the session is too long for the situation. A learner who argues another placement with a reason defends the choice. Which problem to fix first, the content for a phone and the reasons are judged. Plan: Arbeitsauftrag 1, what problems arise, what users expect, which content makes sense.

### Reference: The problem to fix first

**Reference answer.** 1 · One 40-minute video, no pause points

| Option | In the reference answer? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · One 40-minute video, no pause points | Yes | Reference choice: it cannot be used in a few minutes at all, so the learner leaves before learning anything. |
| 2 · An 1,800-word text page | No | Defends: far too long for a phone, though a learner can scroll and stop. |
| 3 · A shrunk desktop menu | No | Defends: hard to hit with a thumb on a moving train. |
| 4 · A nine-column table | No | Slows: affects only the lessons with a table. |
| 5 · Progress missing on the laptop | No | Later: noticed only when the device changes. |
| 6 · Searching again on the laptop | No | Same reading as the row above. |
| 7 · A twelve-question quiz that loses answers | No | Defends: it punishes leaving, which a commuter must do. |
| 8 · Videos with no captions | No | Defends: unusable in a quiet carriage, though readable text remains. |

*Trainer note.* Elements 1, 2, 3, 7 and 8 all defend; 4, 5 and 6 come later. Element 1 is the reference because a learner with five minutes cannot use a 40-minute video at all. Look at the reason: it should say why this comes first for someone on the move. The plan's goal is to develop an understanding of the context of use.

:::note 1.1 · Why does it come first, and what does a learner on the move expect?
**Model answer (an example).** A 40-minute video comes first because a learner on the move has a few minutes, so nothing in the lesson is usable. Such a learner expects speed, simplicity and clarity: something short that works at once, that can be stopped and picked up later, and that makes sense with one hand and many distractions.

**Why.** The reason must say why this problem comes before the others for a learner with little time, and name what such a learner expects (Materi A1).

**Look for.**
- Says why it comes before the others.
- Names what a learner on the move expects.
:::

:::note 1.1 · Which content makes sense on a phone, and which does not?
**Model answer (an example).** On the phone: short units of three to five minutes with one objective (fact 1), a quick quiz of three questions that keeps its answers (fact 7), and videos with captions (fact 8). Better on a laptop: the nine-column table and long reading with notes (facts 2 and 4), and the project work with a file upload, because they need a large screen and a long sitting.

**Why.** The plan asks which content makes sense. A good answer separates what fits a short session on a small screen from what needs a large screen and a long sitting (Materi A1, A2).

**Look for.**
- Names content for the phone and content for the laptop.
- Gives the reason.
- Names the fact each answers.
:::

## Block 1.2 · model answers (UE3)

*Block 1.2: Deciding on a mobile UX strategy: three options · Optional · about 8 min.*

### Reference: Three options for a mobile UX strategy

**Reference answer.** Reference order: A (Shorten the content significantly for mobile (microlearning)), then C (Develop a separate mobile UX), then B (Adopt the desktop version as it is)

| Option | In the reference answer? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A · Shorten the content significantly for mobile (microlearning) (€15,000, 5 weeks) | Yes | It fits the context of use: short sessions, one objective each; the risk is fragmentation of knowledge. Reference ratings: User value High, effort Mid (by the printed cost), risk Mid; position 1. |
| B · Adopt the desktop version as it is (€3,000, 1 weeks) | Yes | Cheapest, but it ignores the context of use and the problem stays. Reference ratings: User value Low, effort Low (by the printed cost), risk Mid; position 3. |
| C · Develop a separate mobile UX (€42,000, 8 weeks) | Yes | It can fit the phone well, but doubles the upkeep and strains the limited budget. Reference ratings: User value Mid, effort High (by the printed cost), risk High; position 2. |

*Trainer note.* Mobile is not a smaller desktop: the context of use decides.

:::note 1.2 · Justify your choice: develop a mobile strategy
**Model answer (an example).** I put A first: mobile use is rising and learners have only a few minutes, so short units fit the context of use, at €15,000 and five weeks. The risk is that knowledge is cut into pieces, which I would manage by giving each unit one objective. C comes second: it could fit well but costs €42,000 and doubles the upkeep. B comes last: it is cheap but ignores the context, so the drop-out stays.

**Why.** The plan's goal is to develop a mobile strategy. A different first choice defends if the reason names the context of use and the budget.

**Look for.**
- Says what each option gives a learner on the move.
- Names the context of use or the budget.
:::

# UE4 · Coaching: transition from Level 1 to Level 2

## Coaching Focus: Mobile UX = Understanding Context (UE4)

**The plan's coaching points.**

- Mobile is not a "smaller desktop"
- Context of use determines design
- Microlearning as an answer to limited attention
- Cross-device as a strategic advantage

**Reflection questions.**

- When does the user use which device?
- Which content belongs on mobile – and which does not?
- Where do breaks in the user experience occur?

| 5 | 5 | Open questions; set the briefing for UE5. |

# UE7 · Feedback round

## Feedback with a Strategic Perspective (UE7)

**Preparation (from the plan).**

- Presentation of the solutions

**Level 3 input: ask these questions.**

- Which measure improves mobile use the most?
- Where is mobile UX underestimated?
- Which decision has the greatest scaling effect?

**Discussion.**

- Mobile vs. desktop
- UX as a context problem

**How to run the unit (about 45 minutes).**

| Step | Minutes | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 20 | Each group presents for about five minutes. The focus is the reasoning, not the result: why this first priority, what was left out. |
| 2 | 15 | Put the Level 3 input questions to the whole room, one at a time. Ask for the answer in business terms: what it moves (completion, retention, engagement, cost). |
| 3 | 10 | Run the discussion. Close with one sentence: the same measures, ordered under a different goal, give a different plan. That is the step to Level 3. |

## Transition from Level 2 to Level 3 (Übergang)

Level 2 asks a participant to decide among measures. Level 3 asks them to decide as a manager, between goals that pull apart. The day's conflict is: **Conflicting goals: mobile against desktop, content against fit to the situation** (Materi B2).

**A participant is ready for Level 3 (Route 2, Task 2) when:**

1. the Core of Task 1 is done and the reason for position one names a reach, a cost or a dependency;
2. the missing information is specific and comes with a way to find it out;
3. they can say the day's conflict in one sentence and name what loses when each side wins.

**If a participant is not there yet:**

- Ask for the reason of position one again, using the “How to decide” box of Materi A5.
- Pair them for UE8 with someone who is ready, for the brief and the first block.
- Give them the “In plain words” box of Materi B1 before the brief; Route 2 does not need Route 1 answers.

Route 2 does not require Route 1: a participant may start the transfer project without having finished Task 1, and use their own answers in the memo if they have them.

## Block 3.1 · model answers (UE9)

*Block 3.1: The mobile strategy, microlearning, cross-device and testing · Core · about 10 min.*

### Reference: The mobile learning strategy

**Reference answer.** One platform, designed from the phone context first: short units on the phone, depth on the desktop, one account

| Option | In the reference answer? | Why |
|---|---|---|

### Reference: The microlearning rules

**Reference answer.** Each unit has exactly one learning objective; A unit takes three to seven minutes; Each unit works alone but shows its place in the course; A short check at the end of each unit; A “continue” card that remembers where the learner stopped

| Option | In the reference answer? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Each unit has exactly one learning objective | Yes | Focus: the learner knows what the unit is for. |
| A unit takes three to seven minutes | Yes | Fits a short break. |
| Each unit works alone but shows its place in the course | Yes | Answers the risk of fragmentation. |
| A short check at the end of each unit | Yes | Shows the learner what they took away. |
| A “continue” card that remembers where the learner stopped | Yes | Makes returning easy. |
| Cut long lessons into five-minute pieces at arbitrary points | No | Rejected: pieces with no objective do not teach. |
| Units that only work if the previous unit is open | No | Rejected: a unit must stand alone. |
| One-hour videos with chapter markers | No | Rejected: still a long sitting. |
| A forced daily streak | No | Rejected: pressure, not microlearning (Day 11). |

*Trainer note.* At least three; any three of the five reference rules defend if each makes a unit finishable in a break and keeps it tied to an objective and to the course. The rejected ones cut without an objective, create dependencies or keep the long sitting. Plan: item 2, microlearning concept.

### Reference: The cross-device placement

**Reference answer.** Phone first: items 1, 4 · Desktop first: items 2, 5 · The same on both: items 3, 6 · Not needed: items 7, 8

| Item | Reference reading |
|---|---|
| 1 · A three-minute unit with one objective | Phone first. Made for short sessions. |
| 2 · Long reading with notes in the margin | Desktop first. Needs a large screen and a long sitting. |
| 3 · Progress and bookmarks | The same on both. Must be identical on every device. |
| 4 · A quick quiz of three questions | Phone first. Fits a short break. |
| 5 · A project with a file upload and a long form | Desktop first. Needs a keyboard and time; open item: a phone can start it. |
| 6 · The same login and the same lesson names | The same on both. Consistency across devices. |
| 7 · A sidebar with twenty links | Not needed. Complexity on every device. |
| 8 · A separate phone-only course catalogue | Not needed. A second product that splits the experience. |

*Trainer note.* There is no single right grid. Item 5 is the open one (desktop first, or the same on both with a phone start). Items 7 and 8 are not needed. Each bin must be used at least once. A good approach sets what is phone first, desktop first and identical everywhere. Plan: item 3, cross-device UX approach.

:::note 3.1 · Why this strategy, and not the others?
**Model answer (an example).** I design one platform from the phone context first: mobile use is growing and learners have minutes, so short units belong on the phone, and the depth stays on the desktop. One account keeps progress whole across devices.

**Why.** The reason must rest on the context of use, the budget and the experience across devices (Materi B1).

**Look for.**
- Gives a reason about the context of use.
- Names the budget or the upkeep.
- Mentions the experience across devices.
:::

:::note 3.1 · How will you test and keep optimising on real phones?
**Model answer (an example).** Each quarter I test with eight learners on their own phones, some of them on the move, doing three tasks while someone watches. Each month I watch drop-out split by device, and I adjust the units that lose the most learners, then repeat.

**Why.** The plan asks for a testing and optimisation strategy: tests on real phones, a figure by device, and a loop (Materi A3, B1).

**Look for.**
- Names a test on real phones.
- Names a figure by device.
- Says how often it repeats.
:::

## Block 3.2 · model answers (UE10)

*Block 3.2: Measures, risk, a decision under uncertainty, and what you give up · Core · about 10 min.*

### Reference: Three prioritised measures

**Reference answer.** D1 Microlearning restructure of the core courses · D2 A phone-first responsive redesign with synchronised progress · D3 Continuous mobile testing and device-level analytics. Total €155,000 of €190,000; the longest takes 16 weeks.

| Option | In the reference answer? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| D1 · Microlearning restructure of the core courses (€55,000, 14 weeks) | Yes | Reference pick: answers the context of use directly. Position 1. |
| D2 · A phone-first responsive redesign with synchronised progress (€70,000, 16 weeks) | Yes | Reference pick: makes the units work on a phone and keeps the experience whole. Position 2. |
| D3 · Continuous mobile testing and device-level analytics (€30,000, 10 weeks) | Yes | Reference pick: shows whether the others work, and closes the data gap. Position 3. |
| D4 · A diary study of when and where learners use which device (€20,000, 8 weeks) | No | Defends in place of D3: it gives the real context, though it does not track change over time. |
| D5 · Native iOS and Android apps (€120,000, 24 weeks) | No | Rejected for now: the highest cost, two products to keep up, and a one-way door. |
| D6 · A separate mobile site with reduced content (€45,000, 12 weeks) | No | Rejected: two products, and the experience breaks when the device changes. |
| D7 · Daily push reminders (€15,000, 6 weeks) | No | Rejected: adds interruptions and does not fix the content. |

*Trainer note.* Reference order: D1, D2, D3 (€155,000 of €190,000, longest 16 weeks). D1 first because the context of use is the main problem. D3, D1, D2 also defends: measure by device first, since the data is missing. D1, D2, D4 defends if the reason names the real context.

### Reference: The biggest risk: fragmentation of knowledge

**Reference answer.** Fragmentation: the short units did not add up, and learners gained pieces of knowledge, not understanding.

| Option | In the reference answer? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Fragmentation: the short units did not add up, and learners gained pieces of knowledge, not understanding. | Yes | The fragmentation pole. Action: every unit names its place in the course, with an end-of-course check; notice it in results on the final task. |
| The two experiences drifted apart: phone and desktop got different names, menus and progress. | No | Action: one component set and one owner; notice it in learners who switch and then leave. |
| We designed for an imagined context, not the real one, because we never watched learners on the move. | No | Action: test on real phones in real settings; notice it in tasks that fail. |
| The cost grew: two products had to be kept up on a limited budget. | No | Action: one platform and a cap on the budget; notice it in cost per unit after the first quarter. |
| A sync error showed wrong progress and learners lost trust in the platform. | No | Action: test the sync before release; notice it in support questions about progress. |

*Trainer note.* Any of the five defends with a reason and an action. The action should cost less than the risk; ask when the team would notice it. These five are the typical answers of a premortem (Klein 2007). The plan names the risk of fragmentation of knowledge.

:::note 3.2 · Why does the first measure go first?
**Model answer (an example).** D1 goes first: the wrong context of use is the main problem, and microlearning answers it directly, since learners on the move have minutes. Context leads to design and design to usage: with the units in place, D2 makes them work on a phone and keeps progress whole across devices, and D3 shows with tests and data by device whether it worked. The three cost €155,000 of the €190,000.

**Why.** The plan's rationale: context → design → usage. A different first measure defends if the reason says how it fits the context of use (Materi B1).

**Look for.**
- Says how it fits the context of use.
- Says why the others can wait.
:::

:::note 3.2 · What do you do about that risk?
**Model answer (an example).** Every unit names the course objective it serves and ends with a short check, and each course ends with a check across its units; if the final-task results of learners who used mostly phone units fall below the others' by the end of month 4, I add a summary unit.

**Why.** One action, and a time when the risk would be noticed (Materi B2, B3).

**Look for.**
- One action.
- Says when the risk would be noticed.
:::

:::note 3.2 · One decision you make under uncertainty (without the data by device)
**Model answer (an example).** I decide … I decide to split the data by device first and to build microlearning units for the twenty most-used lessons (D1) before I redesign the whole platform. I do not know … I do not know how large the share of phone learners really is. I reverse if … (a figure) … by … (a time) I reverse if, by the end of month 3, drop-out on phones has not fallen from 50% to below 42% for the learners who use the new units, and I look at the layout before I cut more content.

**Why.** Four parts: what you decide, what you do not know, what would make you reverse it (a figure) and by when, and what you give up (Materi B3).

**Look for.**
- States the decision in one sentence.
- Names a specific unknown.
- Gives a reversal condition with a figure and a time.
:::

:::note 3.2 · What do you give up or postpone?
**Model answer (an example).** I postpone the native apps (D5) and the daily push reminders (D7) this year.

**Why.** Giving up nothing means you have not decided (Materi B3).

**Look for.**
- Names something real.
- Names who would miss it.
:::
