# Trainer guide

## How to use this document

- **Before the day:** read the lesson plan above and the model solution of the case study (UE5–6). Decide which Optional cards and blocks your group will skip.
- **During the day:** use the section of the unit you are in (UE4 coaching, UE6 debrief, UE7 feedback round, UE10 debrief). Show a model solution only after the participants have worked.
- **Reference answers:** they use the facts and the numbers printed in the Task document. Figures that the plan does not print are Case assumptions. Effort is rated by the printed cost rule taught in Materi A5; user impact and risk are judgements.
- **What is not here:** the Materi (the study material) and the Task (the worksheet) are separate documents and are handed out. This one is not.

## Assessment criteria (Bewertungskriterien)

Use these criteria to read what participants hand in and to give feedback in words. They are the same on every day; the reference answers below say what meets them on this day.

| Criterion | Level | Meets | Partly | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The user's side** | 1 | Describes and sorts from the learner's side; every statement points at a printed fact. | Mostly from the learner's side; some statements are about the design only, or no fact is named. | Describes the platform, not the learner; no fact named. |
| **Weighing under limits** | 2 | Rates user impact, effort and risk with a reason for each; effort follows the printed cost rule; budget and time are checked, or the overrun is explained. | Ratings given, reasons thin, or effort does not follow the rule. | Ratings missing or without reasons; the limits are ignored. |
| **Prioritising** | 2 | Gives a reason for position one (reach, cost or what the others depend on); the order fits the ratings. | An order is given; the reason covers the whole set or is generic. | No order, or no reason. |
| **Missing information** | 2 | Names something specific that would change the decision, and how to find it out. | Names a gap but not how to find it out. | “More data” with nothing specific. |
| **Conflict of goals** | 3 | Names the day's conflicting goals, says who or what loses, and justifies the order. | Names the conflict; the loser is not named. | Presents a plan in which everything wins. |
| **Decision under uncertainty** | 3 | Decides in one sentence, names an unknown, gives a reversal figure and a time, and says what is given up. | Decides; the reversal or the give-up is missing or vague. | Does not decide, or gives up nothing. |

**How to give feedback.** Say what is strong, name one criterion that is only partly met, and give one concrete next step. A choice that differs from the reference is not an error if the reason is clear and refers to a printed fact.

# UE2–3 · Level 1 tasks

## Block 1.1 · model answers (UE2)

*Block 1.1: Eight findings: Orientation, Understanding or Motivation? · Core · about 13 min.*

### Reference: Eight findings into Orientation, Understanding, Motivation

**Reference answer.** Orientation: 14 equal tiles on the dashboard; Yesterday's course is four clicks away; No “Next lesson” button · Understanding: One 500-word block of text; Technical terms without explanation; Very long sentences · Motivation: No progress bar; No message after a quiz

| Item | Reference reading |
|---|---|
| 14 equal tiles on the dashboard | Orientation. The learner cannot tell where to start: orientation. |
| Yesterday's course is four clicks away | Orientation. The learner cannot find where they stopped: orientation. |
| No “Next lesson” button | Orientation. The learner is not told what comes next: orientation. |
| One 500-word block of text | Understanding. The learner has to dig the structure out of the text themselves: understanding. |
| Technical terms without explanation | Understanding. The learner stops at words they do not know: understanding. |
| Very long sentences | Understanding. Long sentences cost attention that is then missing for learning: understanding. |
| No progress bar | Motivation. The learner cannot see they are getting somewhere: motivation, through progress. |
| No message after a quiz | Motivation. The learner gets no sign of success or failure: motivation, through feedback. |

*Trainer note.* The sort is objective: each finding breaks one of the three questions taught in Materi A3. Some findings touch two areas (a wall of text also hides where the main point is); the test is which question the printed phrase breaks first. The 40% drop-out figure is deliberately not a finding to sort: it is where the three areas end up. The second part of the block (the one finding that would make a learner give up first) is judged: any of the eight defends if the reason is written from the learner's side. Plan: Arbeitsauftrag 1, categories Orientation, Understanding, Motivation.

:::note 1.1 · The finding that would make a learner give up first
**Model answer (an example).** On my first visit I see 14 identical tiles and nothing that says what to do first. I would not know where to begin, and I would leave before I had even started a lesson.

**Why.** Any of the eight findings defends. What the plan asks for is the user's perspective (“Versetzen Sie sich in einen Lernenden”): the reason must say what the learner cannot do or feel, not that something looks wrong.

**Look for.**
- Written from the learner's side (“I would not know…”), not about the design.
- Names a concrete consequence (leaving, getting lost, giving up).
- Refers to the finding chosen, not to the platform in general.
:::

## Block 1.2 · model answers (UE3)

*Block 1.2: What the 40% shows and does not show · Optional · about 8 min.*

On Day 1, UE3 uses the rating part of Block 2.2; its reference answers are under Block 2.2 below. Block 1.2 is Optional.

### Reference: What the 40% shows and does not show

| Item | Reference reading |
|---|---|
| 40 of every 100 learners who start a course do not finish it. | The number shows this. It only restates the number. |
| Learners leave because the lessons are too hard. | The number does not show this. A drop-out rate counts who left, not why. |
| A progress bar would bring the number down. | The number does not show this. A number does not predict the effect of a measure. |
| More than one in three learners who start do not finish. | The number shows this. 40 of 100 is more than a third (about 33). |
| The 40 who leave are the learners who find the content boring. | The number does not show this. It says nothing about feelings or reasons. |

*Trainer note.* Anyone who ticks “shows” for a reason or a remedy has read a cause into a symptom. This is the coaching point of the plan (“Daten zeigen Symptome, nicht Ursachen” appears on Day 16; on Day 1 the equivalent is “dealing with incomplete user information”).

:::note 1.2 (Optional) · What you would need to find out why learners leave
**Model answer (an example).** I would interview some of the 40 who left and look at the lesson where most of them stop, because the number only says that they left, not why.

**Why.** A good answer names a source that could show why (interviews, a usability test, data per lesson), not a measure.

**Look for.**
- Names at least one source of evidence about the learners themselves.
- Does not turn the number into a cause.
:::

# UE4 · Coaching: transition from Level 1 to Level 2

## Coaching Focus: From Interface to Systems Thinking (UE4)

**The plan's coaching points.**

- UX is not a design problem, but a decision problem
- Difference: "designing beautifully" vs. "designing effectively"
- How does UX influence learning success?
- Prioritization under conflicting goals
- Dealing with incomplete user information
- Think in user flows instead of individual screens

**Reflection questions.**

- Which decision really improves learning?
- What is "nice to have" vs. "critical"?
- Which UX decision has a business impact?

**How to run the unit (about 45 minutes).**

| Step | Minutes | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 5 | Say the coaching focus in one sentence: “From Interface to Systems Thinking.” Say that in the next unit they will not describe, they will decide. |
| 2 | 10 | Pairs talk through the reflection questions; the notes go into Block 1.3 (never scored). |
| 3 | 15 | Collect two or three answers. Link each to a fact or a finding from Block 1.1, so that the answer is about this platform and not about UX in general. |
| 4 | 10 | Bridge: Level 1 was reading the platform from the user's side. Level 2 asks you to rate and order measures under a budget and a deadline, and to say what information you are missing. Show the first step of Block 2.2. |
| 5 | 5 | Open questions; set the briefing for UE5. |

## Block 1.3 · model answers (UE4)

:::note 1.3 (Optional) · Coaching reflection
**Model answer (an example).** Showing learners where they are and what comes next improves learning more than any new feature, because without it they never reach the content. Critical: a clear path and visible progress. Nice to have: a new look or extra courses. Completion: a clear path lets learners finish, and finishing is what the platform earns from.

**Why.** Reflection is never scored. The plan's coaching points: UX is not a design problem but a decision problem; designing beautifully against designing effectively; thinking in user flows instead of single screens. Use the learners' notes as the start of the live discussion.
:::

# UE5–6 · Case study (2 UE)

## The plan's model solution: core logic (UE5–6)

Show this only after the participants have worked, at the end of UE6.

- **Main problem:** lack of structure & orientation
- **Measures (as the plan lists them):**
  - Define learning paths (greatest impact)
  - Modularize content
  - Integrate a progress indicator
  - Reduce cognitive load

**Rationale (plan):** Impact > Effort > User impact

## Block 2.1 · model answers (UE5–6)

*Block 2.1: Three main causes of the drop-out · Optional · about 7 min.*

### Reference: Three main causes

**Reference answer.** Learners have no clear path through a course: what to do first, next and last. · Lessons are hard to follow: long, unstructured text with unexplained terms. · Learners cannot see their progress or whether they are succeeding.

| Option | In the reference answer? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Learners have no clear path through a course: what to do first, next and last. | Yes | The case names it (“no clear learning path”), and findings 1 to 3 show it. |
| Lessons are hard to follow: long, unstructured text with unexplained terms. | Yes | The case names it (“content hard to understand”), and findings 4 to 6 show it. |
| Learners cannot see their progress or whether they are succeeding. | Yes | Findings 7 and 8 show it (no progress bar, no message after a quiz). |
| The visual style of the platform looks dated. | No | No printed finding says so; it is about the surface, not the experience. |
| The course catalogue is too small. | No | Nothing in the case says learners leave for lack of courses. |
| The subscription price is too high. | No | The case mentions no price complaint. |

*Trainer note.* The plan's model solution names the main problem as a lack of structure and orientation. A learner who swaps one cause for another and points at a printed fact (a finding or a case line) defends the choice; a cause with no printed evidence does not.

:::note 2.1 (Optional) · Why these three causes
**Model answer (an example).** The case itself says there is no clear learning path, and findings 1 to 3 show that learners cannot tell where to start or what comes next.

**Why.** A cause is defended by a printed fact. The plan's model solution names the main problem as a lack of structure and orientation.

**Look for.**
- Points at a printed finding or a case line.
- Does not offer a cause the case never mentions.
:::

## Block 2.2 · model answers (UE5–6)

*Block 2.2: Four measures, rated and ordered · Core · about 17 min.*

### Reference: Choose four measures, rate them, order them

**Reference answer.** Define learning paths; Split lessons into short units; Add a progress indicator; Structure the text of each lesson. Total €47,000 of €50,000.

| Option | In the reference answer? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Define learning paths | Yes | Answers a printed finding (orientation). Effort High by the printed cost €18,000. Reference ratings: user impact High, risk Mid. |
| Split lessons into short units | Yes | Answers a printed finding (understanding). Effort Mid by the printed cost €14,000. Reference ratings: user impact Mid, risk Mid. |
| Add a progress indicator | Yes | Answers a printed finding (motivation). Effort Low by the printed cost €6,000. Reference ratings: user impact Mid, risk Low. |
| Structure the text of each lesson | Yes | Answers a printed finding (understanding). Effort Low by the printed cost €9,000. Reference ratings: user impact Mid, risk Low. |
| Points and badges | No | Rejected in the plan's own logic: points and badges are extrinsic and answer none of the three findings areas directly; the short-term effect fades and the cost is Mid. A learner who funds it must say what it does for the learner. |
| Discussion forum | No | Rejected: a new feature, tied to no printed finding, and the most expensive option (€22,000, effort High). |
| New visual style | No | Rejected: it changes the look (UI), not the experience; no printed finding is about the look. |
| Ten new courses | No | Rejected: more content serves a business goal, not a learner's problem; €20,000 for effort High. |
| Interview learners who left, and test one course | No | Defendable, not in the model: it gathers evidence (cost €7,000, effort Low) and answers the “what information are you missing” item. A learner who funds it instead of m4 should say which measure they drop and why. |

*Trainer note.* The plan's model solution orders them: learning paths (greatest impact), modular content, progress indicator, less cognitive load; its rationale line “Impact > Effort > User impact” is read here as: impact first, then what it costs.

:::note 2.2 · Why the first priority goes first
**Model answer (an example).** Learning paths come first because every learner meets the orientation problem before anything else, and the other three measures work better once a path exists.

**Why.** The plan's model solution puts learning paths first (“greatest impact”). A different first priority defends if the reason is concrete.

**Look for.**
- Gives a reason for position one, not for the whole set.
- Refers to the learner's problem, the cost or what the others depend on.
:::

:::note 2.2 · What information you are missing
**Model answer (an example).** I do not know why the 40 learners leave: the findings show what is hard, not what makes a learner decide to stop. I would interview ten who left before spending all of the €50,000.

**Why.** The plan asks it outright (“Welche Information fehlt Ihnen?”). The good answer names an unknown that would change the decision, and a way to find it out.

**Look for.**
- Names a specific unknown, not “more data”.
- Says how it could be found out.
- Connects to the decision (what would change).
:::

## Debrief of the case study (UE6)

| Step | Minutes | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 15 | Groups of three or four compare their four measures and their order. They name where they differ and why. |
| 2 | 15 | Each group gives the reason for its first priority in one sentence. Write the reasons on a board; do not write the measures. |
| 3 | 10 | Show the plan's core logic and the rationale line (“Impact > Effort > User impact”). Ask: where does a different order still defend? |
| 4 | 5 | Ask each participant to write one thing they would now rate differently. |

# UE7 · Feedback round

## Feedback with a Management Perspective (UE7)

**Preparation (from the plan).**

- Groups present their solutions
- Focus: rationale, not result

**Level 3 input: ask these questions.**

- Which decision has the greatest business impact?
- Which UX decision reduces the drop-out rate sustainably?
- Where is the greatest risk?

**Discussion.**

- UX as a competitive factor
- Short-term vs. long-term impact

**How to run the unit (about 45 minutes).**

| Step | Minutes | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 20 | Each group presents for about five minutes. The focus is the reasoning, not the result: why this first priority, what was left out. |
| 2 | 15 | Put the Level 3 input questions to the whole room, one at a time. Ask for the answer in business terms: what it moves (completion, retention, engagement, cost). |
| 3 | 10 | Run the discussion. Close with one sentence: the same measures, ordered under a different goal, give a different plan. That is the step to Level 3. |

## Transition from Level 2 to Level 3 (Übergang)

Level 2 asks a participant to decide among measures. Level 3 asks them to decide as a manager, between goals that pull apart. The day's conflict is: **Conflicting goals: user value, effort and business goals** (Materi B2).

**A participant is ready for Level 3 (Route 2, Task 2) when:**

1. the Core of Task 1 is done and the reason for position one names a reach, a cost or a dependency;
2. the missing information is specific and comes with a way to find it out;
3. they can say the day's conflict in one sentence and name what loses when each side wins.

**If a participant is not there yet:**

- Ask for the reason of position one again, using the “How to decide” box of Materi A5.
- Pair them for UE8 with someone who is ready, for the brief and the first block.
- Give them the “In plain words” box of Materi B1 before the brief; Route 2 does not need Route 1 answers.

Route 2 does not require Route 1: a participant may start the transfer project without having finished Task 1, and use their own answers in the memo if they have them.

# UE8–10 · Transfer project (3 UE)

## What the plan asks and where the worksheet answers it (UE8–10)

The plan's role: You are the Chief UX Officer of an EdTech company.

| Plan item | Where the worksheet answers it |
|---|---|
| 1. UX vision (How should learning be experienced?) | Block 3.1 (the vision) |
| 2. 3 strategic UX decisions | Block 3.1 (three decisions) |
| 3. Prioritized measures roadmap | Block 3.1 (their order: the roadmap) |
| 4. Risk analysis (wrong decisions) | Block 3.2 (the biggest risk and what you do about it) |
| 5. Decision architecture (How will we decide on UX in the future?) | Block 3.2 (who decides, on what evidence) |
| Additional: Make 1 decision despite an incomplete data situation. | Block 3.2 (the decision with its four parts: what you decide, what you do not know, when you reverse, what you give up) |

**The plan's goals for the project.**

- Think like a decision-maker (not a designer)
- Understand UX as a strategic system
- Take responsibility

## Block 3.1 · model answers (UE9)

*Block 3.1: UX vision, three decisions, and their order · Core · about 9 min.*

### Reference: Three strategic decisions

**Reference answer.** Guided learning paths for the three most-taken courses; Progress and feedback on every course page and quiz; A standing user-research programme

| Option | In the reference answer? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Guided learning paths for the three most-taken courses | Yes | Reference pick: €45,000, 16 weeks. It meets a printed finding that every learner meets. |
| Progress and feedback on every course page and quiz | Yes | Reference pick: €25,000, 12 weeks. It meets a printed finding that every learner meets. |
| Rewrite the ten longest lessons as short, structured units | No | Defendable: it meets the Understanding finding, but at €40,000 and 20 weeks it is the costliest fix; it would go next year. |
| A standing user-research programme | Yes | Reference pick: €30,000, 52 weeks. It is the decision that lets later decisions rest on evidence, which the decision-architecture item of the plan asks for. |
| Points, badges and a leaderboard | No | Rejected in the plan's own logic: extrinsic reward, answers no printed finding, and the effect fades; competitors using it is not a reason. |
| AI-driven personal recommendations | No | Rejected: €90,000 is most of the budget, it needs data SkillUp does not have, and learners cannot see why they are shown what they see. |
| Double the marketing budget | No | Rejected: it fixes acquisition, not experience; more learners arriving at the same platform leave at the same rate. |

*Trainer note.* A different set of three defends if the reason is written and the three together answer the vision.

:::note 3.1 · The UX vision
**Model answer (an example).** Learning at SkillUp should feel guided and visible: every learner always knows where they are, what comes next and how far they have come.

**Why.** The plan's question: “Wie soll Lernen erlebt werden?”. A good vision describes the learner's experience, not a feature list.

**Look for.**
- Describes how learning feels or what the learner can do.
- One or two sentences.
- Is not a list of features or a business target alone.
:::

:::note 3.1 · Why the first decision goes first
**Model answer (an example).** Paths come first because every learner meets orientation before anything else. Progress is cheap and builds on the path. Research runs alongside so later decisions rest on what learners actually do.

**Why.** The roadmap is an order with a reason. The model order is paths, progress, research; a different order defends if the reason is concrete.

**Look for.**
- Gives a reason for position one.
- Refers to reach, cost, dependency or evidence.
:::

## Block 3.2 · model answers (UE10)

*Block 3.2: The biggest risk, a decision without complete data, how you decide, and what you give up · Core · about 11 min.*

### Reference: The biggest risk

**Reference answer.** We treat the symptom (drop-out) and miss its cause.

| Option | In the reference answer? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| We treat the symptom (drop-out) and miss its cause. | Yes | The central risk of the whole plan: SkillUp knows what is hard on the screens, not why learners stop. The research programme is the answer to it. |
| Competitors improve faster than we do while we prepare. | No | Defendable as the biggest risk if the learner says what they do about it; the model picks the cause-versus-symptom risk because the plan's own coaching names it (“UX is not a design problem but a decision problem”). |
| Learners do not use the new paths or progress views. | No | Same reading as the row above. |
| The budget is used up before a first result is visible. | No | Same reading as the row above. |
| Management expects the drop-out rate to fall within weeks. | No | Same reading as the row above. |

*Trainer note.* Any of the five defends when the plan to meet it is concrete (what is done, by whom, when). The wrong answer is a risk with no action.

### Reference: Who decides, on what evidence

**Reference answer.** Who: The UX lead together with the product owner. Evidence: Learner interviews; A usability test of the new screens; Drop-out data per lesson.

| Option | In the reference answer? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Who decides: The Chief UX Officer alone | No | Defendable, but one person's taste is exactly what a decision architecture should prevent. |
| Who decides: The UX lead together with the product owner | Yes | UX judgement and product ownership together: neither taste alone nor build convenience. |
| Who decides: A steering group with management | No | Defendable for large budget items; too slow for routine UX decisions. |
| Who decides: Whoever builds the feature | No | Rejected: the builder decides what is easy to build, not what learners need. |
| Evidence: Learner interviews | Yes | Evidence from learners themselves. |
| Evidence: A usability test of the new screens | Yes | Evidence from learners themselves. |
| Evidence: Drop-out data per lesson | Yes | Evidence from learners themselves. |
| Evidence: A comparison with competitors | No | Useful context, not evidence about SkillUp's own learners. |
| Evidence: The opinion of the most senior person in the room | No | Rejected: authority is not evidence. |

*Trainer note.* At least two kinds of evidence are required; the sort of evidence a learner chooses is judged on the reason in the memo, not on matching the model.

:::note 3.2 · What you do about the biggest risk
**Model answer (an example).** I fund the research programme next to the fixes, so that we find out why learners leave and can correct course after the first quarter.

**Why.** A risk with no action is only a worry. The action should be concrete: what is done, and when it is checked.

**Look for.**
- Names an action.
- Says when or how the risk would be noticed.
:::

:::note 3.2 · The decision without complete data
**Model answer (an example).** I decide to build learning paths for the three most-taken courses now. I do not know whether learners leave from lack of direction or from lack of time. I will refocus if, after the first quarter, the drop-out in those courses has not fallen from 40% to below 35%.

**Why.** The frame of Materi B3: what I decide, what I do not know, what would make me reverse and by when. A checkable reversal condition has a figure and a date.

**Look for.**
- States the decision.
- States what is not known.
- Gives a checkable reversal condition with a figure and a time.

**Typical mistakes.**
- A decision with no stated unknown (“we will improve UX”).
- A reversal condition no one could check (“if it does not work”).
:::

:::note 3.2 · What you give up or postpone
**Model answer (an example).** I give up the badge system and the AI recommendations for this year, and I postpone rewriting the ten longest lessons; learners will keep the long lessons for now.

**Why.** The skill of Level 3: a decision that gives up nothing has not decided. The answer names something real that a stakeholder would miss.

**Look for.**
- Names a concrete thing given up or postponed.
- It is something someone would miss, not a throwaway.
:::

## Debrief of the transfer project (UE10)

| Step | Minutes | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 25 | Each participant presents the decision under uncertainty in the four parts: what I decide, what I do not know, when I reverse (a figure and a time), what I give up. Two minutes each; the others ask one question. |
| 2 | 10 | Name the losers: what did each plan give up, and who would miss it? A plan with no loser has not decided. |
| 3 | 10 | Show the model solution of Block 3.1 and 3.2 and the plan's goals: think like a decision-maker, understand the day as a system, take responsibility. Compare, do not correct. |
