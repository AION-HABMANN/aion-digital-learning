# Trainer guide

## How to use this document

- **Before the day:** read the lesson plan above and the model solution of the case study (UE5–6). Decide which Optional cards and blocks your group will skip.
- **During the day:** use the section of the unit you are in (UE4 coaching, UE6 debrief, UE7 feedback round, UE10 debrief). Show a model solution only after the participants have worked.
- **Reference answers:** they use the facts and the numbers printed in the Task document. Figures that the plan does not print are Case assumptions. Effort is rated by the printed cost rule taught in Materi A5; user impact and risk are judgements.
- **What is not here:** the Materi (the study material) and the Task (the worksheet) are separate documents and are handed out. This one is not.

## Assessment criteria (Bewertungskriterien)

Use these criteria to read what participants hand in and to give feedback in words. They are the same on every day; the reference answers below say what meets them on this day.

| Criterion | Level | Meets | Partly | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The user's side** | 1 | Describes and sorts from the learner's side; every statement points at a printed fact. | Mostly from the learner's side; some statements are about the design only, or no fact is named. | Describes the platform, not the learner; no fact named. |
| **Weighing under limits** | 2 | Rates user impact, effort and risk with a reason for each; effort follows the printed cost rule; budget and time are checked, or the overrun is explained. | Ratings given, reasons thin, or effort does not follow the rule. | Ratings missing or without reasons; the limits are ignored. |
| **Prioritising** | 2 | Gives a reason for position one (reach, cost or what the others depend on); the order fits the ratings. | An order is given; the reason covers the whole set or is generic. | No order, or no reason. |
| **Missing information** | 2 | Names something specific that would change the decision, and how to find it out. | Names a gap but not how to find it out. | “More data” with nothing specific. |
| **Conflict of goals** | 3 | Names the day's conflicting goals, says who or what loses, and justifies the order. | Names the conflict; the loser is not named. | Presents a plan in which everything wins. |
| **Decision under uncertainty** | 3 | Decides in one sentence, names an unknown, gives a reversal figure and a time, and says what is given up. | Decides; the reversal or the give-up is missing or vague. | Does not decide, or gives up nothing. |

**How to give feedback.** Say what is strong, name one criterion that is only partly met, and give one concrete next step. A choice that differs from the reference is not an error if the reason is clear and refers to a printed fact.

# UE2–3 · Level 1 tasks

## Block 1.1 · model answers (UE2)

*Block 1.1: Eight facts, the groups affected and three improvements · Core · about 13 min.*

### Reference: Eight facts into Perceivable, Operable, Understandable

**Reference answer.** Perceivable: facts 1, 2, 3 · Operable: facts 4, 5, 6 · Understandable: facts 7, 8

| Item | Reference reading |
|---|---|
| 1 · Light grey 11 px text, contrast 2.3 : 1 | Perceivable. The text is too small and too pale to read well; contrast is below 4.5 : 1. |
| 2 · Videos without captions or transcript | Perceivable. A learner who cannot hear cannot take in the content. |
| 3 · Status shown only by a coloured dot | Perceivable. Information given by colour alone is lost for learners who cannot tell red from green. |
| 4 · An 18 × 18 px “Next” button, 4 px from “Back” | Operable. The target is small and sits close to another: learners with a tremor or limited hand use hit the wrong one. |
| 5 · A quiz that needs dragging, no keyboard way | Operable. Keyboard and switch users cannot answer at all. |
| 6 · Sign-up in 14 steps over 6 screens | Operable. The plan names “many steps to operate”: the learner cannot reach the end of a long sequence. |
| 7 · Long sentences with legal wording | Understandable. The learner cannot follow the instruction. |
| 8 · “Error 4011” with no explanation | Understandable. The learner cannot tell what went wrong or what to do. |

*Trainer note.* The sort is objective: each fact breaks one of the three principles the plan names. Fact 6 can be argued as Understandable (fourteen steps are hard to follow); the test is what the learner cannot do, and the plan files “many steps to operate” under operation. A learner who argues it with a reason defends the choice.

:::note 1.1 · The groups that struggle, and the facts that block them
**Model answer (an example).** Low vision: fact 1 blocks this group, because the text is too small and too pale to read. Deaf and hard-of-hearing learners: fact 2, because the videos can only be heard.

**Why.** Any two groups defend if each is tied to a fact by number and the answer says what the group cannot do.

**Look for.**
- Names a group and a fact by number.
- Says what the group cannot do (read, hear, operate, understand), not only that it is hard.
- Includes the situational limit (bright light, noisy train) in at least one answer, to show that the gain is wider than one group.

**Typical mistakes.**
- Listing groups without a fact.
- Describing a group by its impairment alone instead of by the task it cannot do.
:::

:::note 1.1 · Three improvements
**Model answer (an example).** Raise the text contrast to at least 4.5 : 1 and let the text be enlarged to 200% (facts 1 and 3): learners with low vision and everyone in bright light gain. Add captions and a transcript to the videos (fact 2): deaf learners and learners on a noisy train gain. Give the quiz a keyboard way to answer (fact 5): keyboard and switch users gain.

**Why.** The plan asks for three improvements. Each should start with a verb, name the fact it answers and say who gains.

**Look for.**
- Starts with a verb.
- Names the fact by number.
- Says who gains.
:::

## Block 1.2 · model answers (UE3)

*Block 1.2: Integrating and assessing a UX concept: three options · Optional · about 8 min.*

### Reference: Integrate or expand: three options

**Reference answer.** Reference order: C (increase accessibility), then B (improve the existing UX), then A (new features)

| Option | In the reference answer? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A · Develop new features (€36,000, 10 weeks) | No | Reference ratings: user impact Low (it opens nothing to the groups who drop out), effort High (€36,000 is above €25,000), risk Mid (a new feature can bring new barriers). An expansion that uses all ten weeks. |
| B · Improve the existing UX (€18,000, 6 weeks) | Yes | Reference ratings: user impact Mid (structure, navigation and wording help many, but not those blocked by pictures, sound or a mouse-only quiz), effort Mid, risk Low. An integration. Defends as first or second. |
| C · Increase accessibility (€24,000, 8 weeks) | Yes | Reference ratings: user impact High (it opens the platform to the groups who drop out), effort Mid (€24,000 is under €25,000), risk Low to Mid (a patch could hide a problem). An integration. |

*Trainer note.* Effort follows the printed cost by the rule in Materi A5 (under €10,000 Low, €10,000 to €25,000 Mid, above €25,000 High); user impact and risk are judgements. The plan's goal is to understand integration against expansion: A expands, B and C build inclusion into what exists. B first also defends if the reason says who gains and what it leaves out.

:::note 1.2 · Justify your decision
**Model answer (an example).** I put C first: the groups who drop out are blocked by contrast, keyboard use and missing captions, and only C changes that. B comes second because clearer structure helps everyone, but it does not remove those barriers. A comes last: it adds a feature on top of a platform that part of the audience cannot use.

**Why.** The plan asks for a prioritisation with a justification.

**Look for.**
- Says who gains from the first priority.
- Says whether the choice is an integration or an expansion.
:::

# UE4 · Coaching: transition from Level 1 to Level 2

## Coaching Focus: Inclusion as a Strategic UX Quality (UE4)

**The plan's coaching points.**

- Accessibility is not an add-on, but a core quality
- Why inclusive systems are more successful in the long run
- Integration of UX into processes and the organization
- Evaluation as a continuous task

**Reflection questions.**

- Who is currently being excluded?
- Which UX decision has the greatest reach?
- How do we measure UX quality meaningfully?

**How to run the unit (about 45 minutes).**

| Step | Minutes | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 5 | Say the coaching focus in one sentence: “Inclusion as a Strategic UX Quality.” Say that in the next unit they will not describe, they will decide. |
| 2 | 10 | Pairs talk through the reflection questions; the notes go into Block 1.3 (never scored). |
| 3 | 15 | Collect two or three answers. Link each to a fact or a finding from Block 1.1, so that the answer is about this platform and not about UX in general. |
| 4 | 10 | Bridge: Level 1 was reading the platform from the user's side. Level 2 asks you to rate and order measures under a budget and a deadline, and to say what information you are missing. Show the first step of Block 2.2. |
| 5 | 5 | Open questions; set the briefing for UE5. |

## Block 1.3 · model answers (UE4)

:::note 1.3 · Coaching reflection (never scored)
**Model answer (an example).** Who is excluded today: learners who cannot read small, pale text, hear the videos or use a mouse. Greatest reach: fixing contrast, keyboard use and captions, because it opens every course. Measuring quality: by task success and drop-out for each group, not by the number of features.

**Why.** Reflection is never scored. The plan's coaching points: accessibility is a core quality, not an add-on; inclusive systems are more successful in the long run; evaluation is a continuous task. Use the notes as the start of the live discussion.
:::

# UE5–6 · Case study (2 UE)

## The plan's model solution: core logic (UE5–6)

Show this only after the participants have worked, at the end of UE6.

- **Main problem:** exclusion of certain user groups
- **Measures (as the plan lists them):**
  - clear structure & plain language
  - better operability
  - visual optimization
  - continuous testing

**Rationale (plan):** Accessibility = usage = success

## Block 2.1 · model answers (UE5–6)

*Block 2.1: Four central barriers · Optional · about 7 min.*

### Reference: Four central barriers

**Reference answer.** Small and low-contrast text · mouse-only or drag-only operation · complicated language and unclear instructions · videos without captions or transcripts

| Option | In the reference answer? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The text is small and low in contrast | Yes | Printed in facts 1 and 3; blocks low-vision and colour-blind learners. |
| Content can only be operated with a mouse or by dragging | Yes | Facts 4 and 5; blocks keyboard, switch and tremor users. |
| The language is complicated and instructions are unclear | Yes | Facts 7 and 8; blocks learners with reading difficulties and learners reading in a second language. |
| Videos have no captions or transcripts | Yes | Fact 2; blocks deaf and hard-of-hearing learners. |
| The subscription price is too high | No | A business matter; no printed fact says it stops a group of learners. |
| The course catalogue is too small | No | A content matter; the case says the content is good. |
| The platform has no dark mode | No | A preference, not a barrier in the case. It would not help the groups who drop out. |

*Trainer note.* A central barrier is defended by a printed fact. The plan's model solution names the main problem as the exclusion of certain user groups.

:::note 2.1 · Which printed fact supports your first barrier
**Model answer (an example).** Fact 5: the quiz can only be answered by dragging items, so keyboard and switch users cannot finish it.

**Look for.**
- Points at a fact by number or quotes the case.
- Names the group it blocks.
:::

## Block 2.2 · model answers (UE5–6)

*Block 2.2: Four measures, rated and ordered, and three evaluation methods · Core · about 17 min.*

### Reference: Four measures, rated and ordered

**Reference answer.** M1 clear structure and plain language · M2 better operability · M3 visual optimisation · M5 continuous testing. Total €47,000 of €50,000; longest measure 6 weeks of 10.

| Option | In the reference answer? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| M1 · Clear structure and plain language (€15,000, 6 weeks) | Yes | Answers facts 7 and 8. Effort Mid by the printed cost. Reference ratings: user impact High (readers with difficulties, second-language learners, everyone at sign-up), risk Low. |
| M2 · Better operability (€13,000, 5 weeks) | Yes | Answers facts 4, 5 and 6. Effort Mid. Reference ratings: user impact High (it lets keyboard, switch and tremor users finish), risk Mid (it touches the quiz and the sign-up). |
| M3 · Visual optimisation (€11,000, 4 weeks) | Yes | Answers facts 1 and 3. Effort Mid. Reference ratings: user impact High (one change on every page), risk Low. |
| M4 · Captions and transcripts (€9,000, 4 weeks) | No | Defends: answers fact 2 for deaf and hard-of-hearing learners. Effort Low. A learner who picks it instead of M5 or M1 should name the group and the fact. |
| M5 · Continuous testing (€8,000, 4 weeks) | Yes | Effort Low. Reference ratings: user impact Mid (it finds barriers, it fixes none), risk Low. In the reference set so that the fixes do not decay: accessibility means usage means success only if it stays. |
| M6 · An accessibility toolbar (€12,000, 3 weeks) | No | Rejected: a layer on top that promises to fix problems automatically; the barriers stay. Effort Mid, user impact Low, risk High (a patch hides the problem). |
| M7 · An AI learning buddy (€40,000, 10 weeks) | No | Rejected: adds a feature, opens nothing to excluded groups; effort High (above €25,000). |
| M8 · A new visual style (€25,000, 6 weeks) | No | Rejected: the look; no printed fact asks for it. Effort Mid (€25,000 is up to the limit). |
| M9 · Two more languages (€30,000, 10 weeks) | No | Rejected: reach in another way; effort High, and it does not answer a printed barrier. |

*Trainer note.* The plan's model solution: clear structure and plain language, better operability, visual optimisation, continuous testing; rationale “Accessibility = usage = success”. The four fit the budget and the time. Reference order (one defensible reading): M3 first (one change on every page, answers facts 1 and 3), then M2 (a group that cannot operate cannot finish at all), then M1 (the widest reach, but six weeks of rewriting), then M5. M1 or M2 first defends equally with a reason.

### Reference: Three evaluation methods

**Reference answer.** E1 automated check · E3 usability test with learners with impairments · E4 task success and drop-out by user group (E2 manual check also defends)

| Option | In the reference answer? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| E1 · Automated accessibility check in each release | Yes | Finds some problems quickly; cannot find all. Gives conformance. |
| E2 · Manual check with keyboard alone and a screen reader | Yes | Finds problems that tools miss. Defends in place of E1. |
| E3 · Usability test with learners with impairments | Yes | Shows why a group cannot finish a task: the method that gives reasons. |
| E4 · Task success and drop-out by user group | Yes | Shows how many in each group are blocked: the method that gives counts. |
| E5 · A satisfaction questionnaire (SUS) per group | No | Defends as a compact signal; it does not show where the barrier is. |
| E6 · Counting the features on the platform | No | Rejected: shows how much was built, not who can use it. |

*Trainer note.* Choose at least one method that gives reasons and one that gives counts or conformance. The learner is judged on the reason, not on matching the reference set.

:::note 2.2 · Why the first priority goes first
**Model answer (an example).** M3 goes first: low contrast and status by colour alone are on every page, so one change reaches every learner with low vision or colour blindness, and it costs €11,000 and four weeks. Without it, the other fixes would sit on pages some learners still cannot read.

**Why.** A different first priority defends if the reason names the group it reaches, the cost, or what the others depend on.

**Look for.**
- Gives a reason for position one, not for the whole set.
- Names a group, a cost or a dependency.
:::

:::note 2.2 · What information you are missing
**Model answer (an example).** I do not know how many of our learners need these features, or which groups they are, because the platform does not record it. I would test with five learners with impairments and, with consent, ask all learners about access needs, before I spend the last €3,000.

**Why.** The plan asks “what information are you missing”. A good answer is specific and says how to find it out without guessing; data protection (DSGVO) applies if groups are measured.

**Look for.**
- Names something specific that would change the decision.
- Says how to find it out.
- Does not guess a number.
:::

## Debrief of the case study (UE6)

| Step | Minutes | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 15 | Groups of three or four compare their four measures and their order. They name where they differ and why. |
| 2 | 15 | Each group gives the reason for its first priority in one sentence. Write the reasons on a board; do not write the measures. |
| 3 | 10 | Show the plan's core logic and the rationale line (“Accessibility = usage = success”). Ask: where does a different order still defend? |
| 4 | 5 | Ask each participant to write one thing they would now rate differently. |

# UE7 · Feedback round

## Feedback with a Strategic Perspective (UE7)

**Preparation (from the plan).**

- Presentation of the solutions

**Level 3 input: ask these questions.**

- Which measure increases reach the most?
- Where is inclusion underestimated?
- Which decision has a long-term influence?

**Discussion.**

- Inclusion vs. costs
- UX as social responsibility

**How to run the unit (about 45 minutes).**

| Step | Minutes | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 20 | Each group presents for about five minutes. The focus is the reasoning, not the result: why this first priority, what was left out. |
| 2 | 15 | Put the Level 3 input questions to the whole room, one at a time. Ask for the answer in business terms: what it moves (completion, retention, engagement, cost). |
| 3 | 10 | Run the discussion. Close with one sentence: the same measures, ordered under a different goal, give a different plan. That is the step to Level 3. |

## Transition from Level 2 to Level 3 (Übergang)

Level 2 asks a participant to decide among measures. Level 3 asks them to decide as a manager, between goals that pull apart. The day's conflict is: **Conflicting goals: inclusion against cost, and integration against add-on** (Materi B2).

**A participant is ready for Level 3 (Route 2, Task 2) when:**

1. the Core of Task 1 is done and the reason for position one names a reach, a cost or a dependency;
2. the missing information is specific and comes with a way to find it out;
3. they can say the day's conflict in one sentence and name what loses when each side wins.

**If a participant is not there yet:**

- Ask for the reason of position one again, using the “How to decide” box of Materi A5.
- Pair them for UE8 with someone who is ready, for the brief and the first block.
- Give them the “In plain words” box of Materi B1 before the brief; Route 2 does not need Route 1 answers.

Route 2 does not require Route 1: a participant may start the transfer project without having finished Task 1, and use their own answers in the memo if they have them.

## Block 3.1 · model answers (UE9)

*Block 3.1: Strategy, integration and three measures · Core · about 9 min.*

### Reference: Where accessibility is checked

**Reference answer.** At least three places, so that accessibility does not depend on one person. The core four: requirements, design review, definition of “done”, release testing.

| Item | Reference reading |
|---|---|
| Requirements and user stories | Accessibility is asked for before anything is built. |
| Design reviews | Barriers are caught on the drawing board. |
| Development: the definition of “done” | A feature is not finished until it passes. |
| Content creation: a standard for authors | New lessons are born accessible. |
| Buying third-party tools | A tool can bring barriers in; procurement is a gate. |
| Release testing | Automated and manual checks before every release. |
| A support channel for access problems | Learners can report what the tests missed. |

*Trainer note.* All seven are defensible places.

### Reference: Three measures

**Reference answer.** D1 fix the barriers in the 20 most-used courses · D2 accessibility in the design system and every release · D3 a quarterly test programme. €150,000 of €240,000.

| Option | In the reference answer? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| D1 · Fix the barriers in the 20 most-used courses (€80,000, 22 weeks) | Yes | Reach now: the groups blocked today stay blocked while you wait. |
| D2 · Accessibility in the design system and every release (€40,000, 16 weeks) | Yes | Integration: otherwise new content re-creates the barriers. |
| D3 · A quarterly test programme (€30,000, 52 weeks) | Yes | Evidence by group; the plan asks for an evaluation system. |
| D4 · A plain-language standard and author training (€25,000, 12 weeks) | No | Defends: prevents new inaccessible text. Instead of D3 if the reason says how it will be checked. |
| D5 · An external accessibility audit (€35,000, 8 weeks) | No | Defends as diagnosis, but an audit finds problems and fixes none: the premortem failure “we spent the budget on an audit and had nothing left to fix”. |
| D6 · An accessibility toolbar (€20,000, 4 weeks) | No | Rejected: a layer on top; the barriers stay. |
| D7 · New features for growth (€90,000, 28 weeks) | No | Rejected: an expansion; it reaches no excluded group and uses the largest share of the budget. |

*Trainer note.* Reference order: D1, D2, D3. D1 first because every month of waiting is a month of excluded learners; D2 second so that the fix lasts; D3 third to show the effect by group. D2 first also defends (it removes the dependence on one person).

:::note 3.1 · Your strategy for inclusive UX
**Model answer (an example).** We aim to reach learners with low vision, hearing, motor and reading difficulties, who drop out today. We change first what blocks them: contrast, keyboard use, captions and plain language in the 20 most-used courses. Inclusion is a core quality, not an add-on: a learner who cannot use a lesson is a learner we lose, and each fix helps everyone (captions on a train, contrast in sunlight).

**Why.** The plan asks for an inclusive UX strategy and its integration into existing processes.

**Look for.**
- Names the groups it aims to reach.
- Says what changes first.
- Says why it is a core quality.
:::

:::note 3.1 · Why the first measure goes first
**Model answer (an example).** D1 goes first: it reaches the groups who are blocked today, in the courses most learners open. Each month of waiting costs learners we already have.

**Look for.**
- Names the groups it reaches.
- Says what waiting costs.
:::

## Block 3.2 · model answers (UE10)

*Block 3.2: Evaluation system, risk analysis, a decision under uncertainty, and what you give up · Core · about 11 min.*

### Reference: The evaluation system: KPIs and methods

**Reference answer.** KPIs (at least three): task success by group, drop-out by group, share of pages that meet WCAG 2.2 AA, access problems and time to fix, satisfaction by group. Methods (at least two): a test with learners with impairments, a manual audit, automated checks, analytics by group.

| Option | In the reference answer? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| KPI · Task success by user group | Yes | Shows whether a group cannot finish the main task. |
| KPI · Drop-out by user group | Yes | A symptom of exclusion. |
| KPI · The share of pages that meet WCAG 2.2 AA in an audit | Yes | Measures the product against the standard. |
| KPI · Accessibility problems reported, and the time to fix them | Yes | Shows whether the process works. |
| KPI · Satisfaction (for example SUS) by group | Yes | A compact signal that a group has trouble. |
| KPI · The number of clicks on a toolbar | No | Rejected: counts use of a layer, not access. |
| KPI · The number of features released | No | Rejected: counts what was built, not who can use it. |
| Method · A usability test with learners with impairments | Yes | Gives reasons. |
| Method · A manual audit (keyboard, screen reader) | Yes | Gives conformance and finds what tools miss. |
| Method · Automated checks in each release | Yes | Gives quick, partial conformance. |
| Method · Analytics by group (with a legal basis) | Yes | Gives counts. Check the DSGVO duties before measuring by group. |
| Method · A survey of all learners | No | Defends as one source; it is not by group and shows no barrier. |

*Trainer note.* Choose at least one method that gives reasons (a test with users) and one that gives counts or conformance. The learner is judged on the reason, not on matching the reference set.

### Reference: The biggest risk (costs against benefits)

**Reference answer.** Reference: “Accessibility stayed a project and the platform drifted back.” Any of the five defends with a reason and an action.

| Item | Reference reading |
|---|---|
| Accessibility stayed a project and the platform drifted back | The most common failure; answered by D2 (definition of “done”, checks in every release). |
| Authors ignored the standard, so new content was inaccessible | Answered by a standard and training (D4) and a release check. |
| We bought a toolbar and the barriers stayed | Answered by fixing the source, never by a layer. |
| We spent the budget on an audit and had nothing left to fix | Answered by ordering: fix first, audit later. |
| We could not show a benefit and the budget was cut | Answered by KPIs by group from the first quarter. |

*Trainer note.* The action should cost less than the risk; ask the learner to compare the two.

:::note 3.2 · Who reads the results, how often, and what happens when a KPI is poor
**Model answer (an example).** The accessibility lead reads the group dashboard monthly and the product board reads it each quarter. If task success for any group falls below the rest, the owner of that course fixes the barrier in the next release.

**Why.** A decision architecture names a role, a rhythm and a consequence.

**Look for.**
- Names a role.
- Names a rhythm.
- Says what must be done when a KPI is poor.
:::

:::note 3.2 · What you do about the biggest risk
**Model answer (an example).** I put accessibility into the definition of “done” and the release checks (D2), so it does not depend on a project. The action costs about the price of one measure; a platform that drifts back costs the whole programme.

**Look for.**
- One action.
- Compares its cost with the cost of the risk.
:::

:::note 3.2 · The decision under uncertainty
**Model answer (an example).** I decide to fix contrast, keyboard use and captions in the 20 most-used courses first. I do not know how many of our learners are affected or which groups they are. I reverse if, by the end of the second quarter, task success of the tested learners in those courses has not risen from 60% to 75%.

**Why.** A decision under uncertainty states what you decide, what you do not know, what would make you reverse it (a figure) and by when.

**Look for.**
- States the decision in one sentence.
- Names a specific unknown.
- Gives a reversal condition with a figure and a time.
:::

:::note 3.2 · What you give up or postpone
**Model answer (an example).** I give up the new sales features (D7) for this year and postpone the external audit (D5) to year two. Sales will miss the features they asked for.

**Why.** A plan that gives up nothing has not decided.

**Look for.**
- Names something real that someone would miss.
:::

## Debrief of the transfer project (UE10)

| Step | Minutes | What you do |
